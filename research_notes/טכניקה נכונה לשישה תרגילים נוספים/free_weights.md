# Free-weight exercises: Romanian deadlift (barbell), dumbbell curl, dumbbell shoulder press. Technique spec for a 5-second single-rep loop

Conventions match `reports/טכניקה נכונה לעשרה תרגילים.md`. Any number marked **[illustration default]** is my own geometric choice, not a published figure. The sources below were read in full unless marked "(search summary only)". ExRx (403), Physio-pedia (403) and PubMed (cookie wall) did not open. The NSCA *Exercise Technique Manual* was not accessed.

## Q1. Romanian deadlift (barbell)

### Takeaway
ACE's own technique article confirms the brief. The lift starts standing with the bar at the hips. Feet are hip-width, the knees slightly bent, and the grip is pronated at about shoulder width. The hips hinge back with the bar sliding down the thighs, and the lowering stops where the hamstrings limit it (about the knees, or mid-shin if flexible). The person returns by pressing the hips forward. The "15-20 degree" knee figure is common in coaching sources but I could not trace it to ACE, NSCA or any study. It is defensible, but not authoritative.

### Cited Findings
- Stance and grip: "Maintain a slight bend in the knees with the feet hip-width apart"; "Use a pronated (palms-down) grip to firmly grasp a barbell with the hands approximately shoulder-width apart." — [ACE Technique Series: Romanian Deadlift (Pete McCall, ProSource Jan 2016)](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/prosource/january-2016/5767/ace-technique-series-romanian-deadlift/)
- Hinge: "push the tailbone in the posterior direction to hinge at the hips." — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/prosource/january-2016/5767/ace-technique-series-romanian-deadlift/)
- Bar path: "keep the bar against the body the entire time"; cue the client to "pull the bar into their body and to slide or glide it down their thighs" (quoting Eric Gentilcore). — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/prosource/january-2016/5767/ace-technique-series-romanian-deadlift/)
- Also "Actively keep the bar as close to the legs as possible throughout the motion" and "Stay balanced evenly over the whole foot rather than pushing the hips back more than necessary and shifting to the heels." — [Catalyst Athletics](https://catalystathletics.com/exercise/101/Romanian-Deadlift/)
- Depth: "lower until tension is felt in the back of the thighs", which is usually "about knee height", or "about mid-shin" for clients with more hamstring flexibility. — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/prosource/january-2016/5767/ace-technique-series-romanian-deadlift/)
- Depth (weightlifting context): the knees bend "very slightly to bring the bar as far down the legs as possible without losing any back extension". Even when mobility allows, "still stop just short of touching" the floor. — [Catalyst Athletics](https://catalystathletics.com/exercise/101/Romanian-Deadlift/)
- Spine: "Lift the chest and pull the shoulder blades down toward the back pockets to maintain extension of the spine." — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/prosource/january-2016/5767/ace-technique-series-romanian-deadlift/)
- Head: "keep his or her chin tucked into the neck as if holding an egg". The client looks toward the floor, not at a mirror. Looking in the mirror is listed as an error because it strains the neck. — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/prosource/january-2016/5767/ace-technique-series-romanian-deadlift/)
- Knees stay fixed: "Do NOT allow the client to round the back or extend the knees while lowering the weight." Common error: "clients often bend their knees during the RDL, as if they were performing a squat." — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/prosource/january-2016/5767/ace-technique-series-romanian-deadlift/)
- Return: "push both heels into the floor, press the hips forward and pull back on the knees while keeping a long spine." — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/prosource/january-2016/5767/ace-technique-series-romanian-deadlift/)
- Return at the bottom: "Stay braced tightly so as you change directions at the bottom, you don't allow any softening of the back extension." — [Catalyst Athletics](https://catalystathletics.com/exercise/101/Romanian-Deadlift/)
- Knee angle numbers (search summary only, coaching sources): "about 15-20 degrees" ([Robertson Training Systems / T-Nation](https://robertsontrainingsystems.com/blog/perfecting-the-romanian-deadlift/)); "approximately 15 degrees", held constant ([Physio-pedia](https://physio-pedia.com/Romanian_deadlift), 403 on fetch); "10-15 degree" ([Gravitus](https://gravitus.com/guides/exercises/romanian-deadlift)); "8-10 degrees" ([SimpliFaster](https://simplifaster.com/articles/romanian-deadlifts-technique-programming/)). None of these cites a study for its number.
- Shins: no shin-angle cue in ACE beyond using mid-shin as a depth landmark. Coaching sources say the shins stay "nearly vertical" (search summary only). — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/prosource/january-2016/5767/ace-technique-series-romanian-deadlift/)

### Inferences
- **Camera: true side view.** Every defining cue happens in the sagittal plane: the hips travelling back, the bar staying on the legs, a flat back, the knee angle not changing. A front view hides all of them.
- **Loop timeline [illustration default]:** 0.0–0.4 s standing lockout: bar against the upper thighs, arms straight and vertical, knees soft (~15°). 0.4–2.4 s hinge: the hips move back, the torso tilts forward, and the bar slides down the thighs over mid-foot. 2.4–2.7 s turnaround: bar just below the kneecaps (the "about knee height" general-audience depth, deliberately not mid-shin), torso about 30–45° above horizontal. 2.7–4.6 s return: the hips drive forward and the bar slides back up the thighs. 4.6–5.0 s hold the start pose so the loop closes. ACE's "slowly lower" style supports a lowering phase longer than the rise, but the timing split is my own choice.
- **Line to protect:** the straight line from pelvis through shoulder to head never bends. The knee angle is the same in every frame. The shins stay near-vertical (the knees do not drift forward, and the hips move backward rather than down). The bar stays vertically over mid-foot, which follows from Catalyst's "evenly over the whole foot".
- **Grip [illustration default]:** both hands pronated, thumbs wrapped, hands just outside the thighs at about shoulder width. Arms hang straight and vertical in every frame (the arms do not pull). Shoulder blades are drawn down per ACE. Chin tucked, gaze down and ahead on the floor about 1–2 m forward, since ACE says floor and not mirror.
- **Difference from the existing conventional deadlift loop:** the RDL never starts from the floor, the bar never touches the floor, and the knees do not re-bend at the bottom.
- **Three faults never to show:** (1) a rounded back at the bottom, or the back softening at the turnaround; (2) a squat pattern where the knees bend more as the bar descends and the shins tilt forward; (3) the bar drifting away from the legs, or the weight rocking onto the heels with the toes lifting. Secondary: neck craned up to look forward, or leaning back at lockout.

### Gaps
- No ACE, NSCA or peer-reviewed source for a specific knee angle. 15–20° is coaching consensus only. I would show about 15° as **[illustration default]**.
- ACE's full RDL page in the general Exercise Library was not found. Only the ProSource technique article was read.
- No sourced torso angle at the bottom. 30–45° above horizontal is **[illustration default]**, consistent with bar-at-knee depth.

## Q2. Dumbbell biceps curl (standing)

### Takeaway
Mayo Clinic says the curl may be done standing or sitting, and with one arm or alternating. ACE's library entry is the seated two-arm version, curling both arms "in unison". So **both arms together, supinated (palms forward) from the start** is the simplest standard, and the best version to illustrate. Mayo frames alternating as a variant that adds core/stability demand. The elbows stay close to the body and do not move forward. The wrist stays neutral. Lowering is slow, back to full extension.

### Cited Findings
- Grip: "Grasp two dumbbells with a closed, supinated grip (palms facing forward and thumbs wrapped around the handles)"; arms hang "to your sides, close to your body, with your palms facing forward." — [ACE Seated Biceps Curl](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/44/seated-biceps-curl/)
- Movement: "slowly bend each elbow in unison, bringing the dumbbells towards your chest… without arching your back or moving your elbows forward." "Maintain a neutral wrist position (avoid flexion and extension of your wrists)." — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/44/seated-biceps-curl/)
- Shoulders: "Depress and retract… your scapulae"; "avoid shrugging your shoulders during the lift." — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/44/seated-biceps-curl/)
- Lowering: "gently lower the dumbbells back towards your starting position." — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/44/seated-biceps-curl/)
- Rotating variant: "Turn your palms towards your sides and rotate them forward (or up) during the upward phase", and "rotate your forearms back inward as your forearms reach horizontal". A further variation lets the elbows move forward to curl higher. ACE lists both as **variations**, not the base form. — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/44/seated-biceps-curl/)
- Mayo Clinic (video, via a Care Network site): the curl can be done standing or sitting, and alternating arms adds a core/stability challenge. Hold the dumbbell "palm facing upward", "Slowly curl the weight up by bending your elbow, keeping your elbow close to your body", "Then slowly lower the weight to the starting position". Keep the wrist "straight and rigid", and don't swing the arm or elbow. — [Mayo Clinic video via Beacon Health System](https://www.beaconhealthsystem.org/library/video/video-biceps-curl-with-dumbbell)
- Equipment manual (low authority, search summary only): underhand grip beside the thighs, upper arms against the ribcage, curl to shoulder height, lower slowly. — [Spirit XT485 manual](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/631869/Spirit-Xt485.html?page=23)
- Coaching sources (search summary only): elbows drifting forward shift tension from the biceps to the front delts, and elbow flare usually means the weight is too heavy. — [Rep Fitness](https://repfitness.com/blogs/training/supinated-dumbbell-curls); [Fitness Volt](https://fitnessvolt.com/dumbbell-supinated-curl/)

### Inferences
- **Camera: side view, or a shallow 3/4 from the front-side (~20–30° off pure side) [illustration default].** A true side view shows the two things that matter most: the upper arm staying vertical, and the torso not swinging. The far arm is hidden behind the near one, which is fine with both arms moving in unison, since the arms overlap and read as one. A slight 3/4 also shows that the palm faces forward. Do not use a front view, because forward elbow drift and torso swing are invisible from the front.
- **Standing setup [illustration default]:** feet hip-width, knees soft (not locked), torso vertical, shoulders down and back. ACE's "neutral, unshrugged" cue carries over from the seated version.
- **Loop timeline [illustration default]:** 0.0–0.3 s start: arms fully extended at the sides, palms forward, dumbbells beside the thighs. 0.3–2.0 s curl. 2.0–2.4 s top: forearm close to vertical, dumbbell at about shoulder height/front of the shoulder, upper arm still vertical. 2.4–4.7 s slow lowering. 4.7–5.0 s full extension again. Lowering slower than lifting follows "slowly"/"gently" (ACE, Mayo). The exact split is mine.
- **Top position:** with the elbow held still, the dumbbell reaches roughly shoulder height. The manual says "to shoulder height". ACE says "towards your chest". ACE only allows going higher by moving the elbow forward as a variation, so the base demo stops where the elbow would have to move.
- **Hand detail:** closed grip, thumb wrapped around the handle, wrist straight in line with the forearm (not curled toward the body at the top, not bent back at the bottom).
- **Three faults never to show:** (1) torso swing or leaning back to heave the weight up; (2) the elbows travelling forward/up away from the ribs (the upper arm leaving vertical); (3) dropping the weight fast or stopping short of full extension, i.e. a partial rep. Secondary: flexed or extended wrists, and shrugged shoulders.

### Gaps
- No ACE standing dumbbell curl page was found. The standing stance cues are inferred from the seated page plus Mayo. ExRx (403) was not readable.
- I found no authoritative statement that two-arm-together is "the" standard over alternating. Mayo treats both as valid. Two-arm-together is my recommendation for the demo because ACE's base instruction uses "in unison" and it closes into a symmetric loop.
- No sourced elbow angle at the top. "Forearm near vertical, about 30–40° short of full flexion" is **[illustration default]**.

## Q3. Dumbbell shoulder press: standing vs seated

### Takeaway
Both versions are standard. Library sources describe it standing (Catalyst) or "can be performed standing or sitting" (Physitrack). Rehab-oriented sources (Physitrack) default to seated. Evidence favours **seated with a back rest** as the safest version for a general audience. In Saeterbakken & Fimland, the standing dumbbell press was the least stable condition, and lifters were about 10% weaker standing than seated. Arching the lower back is a named fault. A back rest makes that fault hard to commit. Standing does give more deltoid and core activity. The start position is the dumbbells at shoulder height, palms facing forward, forearms vertical. The press goes straight up to full lockout with the wrists over the shoulders, then comes back down the same path.

### Cited Findings
- Start and finish: "Stand with a dumbbell in each hand at the shoulders with the palms facing approximately forward… Press into a fully locked out, stable overhead position"; "with the upper back tight"; lower "under control to the starting position". — [Catalyst Athletics, Dumbbell Press](https://catalystathletics.com/exercise/830/Dumbbell-Press/)
- Physitrack: "Can be performed standing or sitting". "Hold a pair of dumbbells at your shoulders in the press position, with your palms facing forwards… press the weights overhead ensuring your arms are fully locked out… Lower the dumbbells back down to your shoulders". The grip may be underhand or overhand. — [Physitrack overhead dumbbell press](https://www.physitrack.com/de/exercise-library/how-to-perform-the-overhead-dumbbell-press-exercise)
- Physitrack's seated versions disagree on elbow direction: one says "elbows pointing forward", the other "elbows pointing to the side" (search summary only). — [Physitrack seated, elbows forward](https://us.physitrack.com/home-exercise-video/seated-dumbbell-shoulder-press); [Physitrack seated with dumbbells](https://www.physitrack.com/exercise-library/how-to-perform-the-shoulder-press-seated-dumbbell-exercise)
- ACE's seated shoulder press (barbell version, bench with back rest): "keep the back straight", "Press the feet into the floor, squeeze the stomach muscles", "keep the elbows pointed forward while pressing the bar directly overhead", lower to "about chin-height". — [ACE Seated Shoulder Press](https://acefitness.org/education-and-resources/lifestyle/exercise-library/43/seated-shoulder-press)
- Faults: "Lumbar hyperextension and a rib flare to finish a too-heavy press are faults". Brace "so the lower back doesn't arch into a standing bench press". Dumbbell presses are spotted "at the wrists, not by shoving the elbows" (NSCA-CPT secondary study guide, not NSCA itself). — [NSCA-CPT study guide](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/nsca-cpt/resistance-technique/press-pull-technique)
- Saeterbakken & Fimland 2013, JSCR 27(7):1824–31, n=15 men. The standing dumbbell press 1-RM was about 7% lower than standing barbell and about 10% lower than seated dumbbell. The standing dumbbell press had the highest deltoid EMG: seated dumbbell was 15% lower for the medial deltoid and 24% lower for the posterior deltoid. Conclusion: the most stability-demanding condition gave the highest deltoid activity but the lowest strength. — [abstract via BISp-SURF](https://www.bisp-surf.de/Record/PU201311007613); [PubMed 23096062](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23096062/)
- Core EMG (search summary only): standing and seated dumbbell presses showed similar erector spinae activity, and the authors recommend standing to raise core activation. n=15. — [Saeterbakken & Fimland 2012, PubMed 21877146](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21877146/)
- Coaching (search summary only): "press directly overhead (not forward), so wrists stack directly over shoulders", and lower along the same path. — [Yahoo/syndicated](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/execute-perfect-dumbbell-overhead-press-043000232.html)

### Inferences
- **Recommendation for the app: seated, upright back rest, feet flat.** This is a judgement call. No study measured lumbar load directly, and the erector EMG was similar seated and standing. Two things favour it: the named fault (lumbar hyperextension/rib flare) is visually prevented by the back rest, and standing is the least stable condition. If the app's library already has the standing barbell press (it does: x71), the seated dumbbell version also gives a clear visual contrast.
- **Back-rest angle [illustration default]: about 80–90° (upright, or one notch back from vertical).** No source gives a number. ACE only says "keep the back straight" against the bench. Head, upper back and buttocks touch the pad, mirroring ACE's three-point contact for the seated curl.
- **Camera: front view, or a front 3/4 (~30° off front) [illustration default].** Unlike the barbell press, the defining dumbbell features are in the frontal plane: forearms vertical, dumbbells rising "slightly in" toward each other, no clanking at the top. A pure side view hides the far arm and the inward path. A shallow front 3/4 still shows that the back stays on the pad (no arch). This differs from the side view chosen for the barbell overhead press.
- **Start/turnaround:** start with the dumbbell handles at about shoulder/ear-lobe height, just outside the shoulders. Palms face forward, forearms vertical from the front, elbows under the wrists and slightly in front of the torso (between "forward" and "to the side", i.e. about 30–45° forward of the frontal plane, **[illustration default]**, splitting the Physitrack disagreement). Top: elbows locked (Catalyst/Physitrack "fully locked out"), dumbbells over the shoulders or slightly inside, about a fist apart. They don't touch. Biceps near the ears.
- **Path:** a slight inward arc from shoulder width to above the shoulders, then down the same arc. The wrists stay stacked over the elbows the whole way.
- **Loop timeline [illustration default]:** 0.0–0.3 s start hold; 0.3–2.0 s press; 2.0–2.4 s lockout; 2.4–4.7 s controlled lower; 4.7–5.0 s start pose.
- **Three faults never to show:** (1) arching the lower back off the pad, or ribs flaring at lockout; (2) wrists/forearms not stacked, with the dumbbells drifting forward or out wide, or the elbows flared fully to the sides with the forearms angled; (3) the dumbbells clanking together overhead, or a partial rep that stops short of lockout or bounces off the shoulders. Secondary: shrugged neck at the bottom, or the head thrust forward.

### Gaps
- No source specifies a back-rest angle for the seated dumbbell press. 80–90° is an illustration default.
- No source quantifies the inward path or the gap between the dumbbells at lockout. "About a fist apart, never touching" is an illustration default. "Without clanking" is coaching lore with no citable authority found.
- No direct lumbar-load comparison between seated and standing dumbbell presses was found. The safety ranking is reasoned from stability and the named fault, not measured.
- No Mayo Clinic or ACE general-library page for the dumbbell shoulder press was found.
- **Pose references (as in the earlier report) were not searched for on Wikimedia Commons** in this pass. A generic search turned up only stock sites. A direct Commons search for "Everkinetic" + each exercise is still needed.
