# Correct technique for a 5-second side-view loop: Push-up, Forearm Plank, Pull-up (pronated, strict), Bent-over Barbell Row (overhand)

Note on sources: ExRx.net (403), the Royal Navy pull-up page (403), the NSCA *Exercise Technique Manual* and the FM 7-22 / H2F manual text were **not reachable** in this session. Two ACE Exercise Library pages (push-up, front plank) were read in full. One Human Kinetics excerpt was read in full: Palin & Hartman, *Preparing for the ACFT*, which describes the Army hand-release push-up. One peer-reviewed study (Fenwick, Brown & McGill 2009) was read at abstract level. Everything else is coaching or industry sources, labelled as such. Where only a search snippet supported a claim (not a full page), that is noted.

## 1. Push-up (standard, on the hands)

### Takeaway
Side view: a rigid straight plank from head to heels, pivoting at the toes. Hands are under the shoulders (at mid-chest level), fingers forward. The body lowers until the chest is near or touching the floor, then presses back to fully straight elbows. Sources disagree on elbow angle. Recommend that the illustration show the upper arm about 45° from the torso (the band sources give is 30–60°) and never flared to 90°.

### Cited Findings
- Start (ACE): palms flat, shoulder-width apart, fingers forward or turned slightly inward; weight shifted forward until the **shoulders are directly over the hands** — [ACE Push-Up](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/41/push-up/)
- Body line (ACE): brace the core, squeeze glutes and quads, body straight with **no bend at hips or knees**. Head aligned with the spine, "don't let it drop or lift". Feet together with ankles dorsiflexed (toes tucked, standing on the balls of the feet) — [ACE Push-Up](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/41/push-up/)
- Depth (ACE): lower with a rigid torso "until your chest or chin touches the mat"; press up "until your elbows are fully extended" — [ACE Push-Up](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/41/push-up/)
- Faults named by ACE: low back sagging or hips hiking up, in either phase — [ACE Push-Up](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/41/push-up/)
- **Elbow conflict:** ACE's standard push-up says "let your elbows flare outward as you lower". ACE's alternative (hands forward, elbows close to the sides) "may reduce stress on the shoulder joint" — [ACE Push-Up](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/41/push-up/)
- The US military HPRC says the most pushing force comes with the arms abducted "about 30–60 degrees out from your sides". Elbows tight shift work to the triceps and front deltoid; elbows out shift it to the pecs — [HPRC (Uniformed Services University)](https://hprc-online.org/physical-fitness/training-performance/change-your-hand-position-optimize-your-workouts)
- Coaching sources (snippet level, not primary): elbows about 45° from the torso, kept below about 60°; hands at mid-chest level, just outside the shoulders. Hands placed higher than the shoulders is called the worst position for shoulder impingement. Elbows flared to about 90° is the most often cited error — [Built With Science](https://builtwithscience.com/fitness-tips/perfect-push-up-form/)
- Army standard (ACFT hand-release push-up): index fingers "inside the broadest part of your shoulders"; "a fairly straight line from the head to the heel"; feet "no more than a boot's width apart". Chest, hips and thighs reach the floor "in unison". The only rest allowed is the top position with arms extended and body straight — [Human Kinetics excerpt, Palin & Hartman, *Preparing for the ACFT*](https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/hand-release-push-up-arm-extension-hrp)
- Research context: a common taxonomy puts a wide hand width at about 150% of shoulder width and a narrow one at about 50%. EMG results on which width works the chest more conflict between studies (search-snippet level) — [Contreras/Schoenfeld PDF](https://www.lookgreatnaked.com/articles/biomechanics_of_the_Pushup.pdf); [J Phys Ther Sci 2015](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jpts/27/8/27_jpts-2015-296/_article/-char/en)

### Inferences
- **Side-view keyframes for an illustrator:**
  - **Top (frame 0 and the final frame):** arms vertical, elbows straight but not hyperextended. The wrist is directly below the shoulder. Ears, shoulders, hips, knees and ankles sit on one straight line, sloping down from head to feet at roughly 15–20°, depending on arm length (this angle is my geometric estimate, not sourced).
  - **Bottom (about the 2.5 s mark):** chest 1–3 cm above the floor (or just touching). The elbow angle is about 90° or less. The forearm stays roughly vertical, with the elbow directly above the wrist or slightly behind it. Body line still straight.
  - **Path:** the whole body rotates about the toes as one rigid lever. The hips must not lag behind the chest, and must not lead it.
- From the side, a 45° elbow angle shows as the elbow pointing back toward the hips, with only part of the upper arm hidden behind the torso. A 90° flare would put the elbow pointing straight out sideways, so in a pure side view the upper arm would look foreshortened. A 3/4 side view shows the angle more clearly.
- Safest for a general audience: hands under the shoulders, slightly wider than shoulder width; elbows about 45°; chest near the floor, with no requirement to touch.
- **Three mistakes the demo must never show:**
  1. Hips sagging (a banana-shaped back) or piking up.
  2. Elbows flared to about 90° in a "T" shape, or hands placed forward of the shoulders.
  3. Head dropping, so the chin reaches the floor before the chest, or the neck craned up.

### Gaps
- I found no reachable primary text (FM 7-22/H2F, NSCA) that states an elbow angle in degrees. The 45° figure comes from coaching sources and from HPRC's 30–60° band.
- ACE's own standard push-up allows the elbows to flare, which conflicts with the coaching consensus. A reconciled authoritative statement was not found.

## 2. Forearm plank (hold)

### Takeaway
Static side view: forearms on the floor with the elbows directly under the shoulders, palms down. Toes tucked under. Knees straight. One straight line from ears to heels, with the hips level: not sagging, not piked. Shoulders are not shrugged. Head neutral, eyes looking at the floor.

### Cited Findings
- ACE front plank: elbows close to the sides and **directly under the shoulders**, palms down. Legs extended, toes pulled toward the shins. Core braced — [ACE Front Plank](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/32/front-plank/)
- ACE: "keep your shoulders directly over your elbows. Don't shrug your shoulders"; hips must not hike up; "low back from sagging or arching"; knees straight. Keep breathing; hold at least 5 s — [ACE Front Plank](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/32/front-plank/)
- ACE warning: stop if there is pain in the low back — [ACE Front Plank](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/32/front-plank/)
- Coaching sources: body in a straight line supported on the forearms and toes, with the elbows directly under the shoulders — [Adam Kemp Fitness](https://adamkempfitness.com/?p=29231); [Yoga Matters](https://www.yogamatters.com/blogs/pose-library/forearm-plank)

### Inferences
- **Side-view spec:**
  - The upper arm is vertical, about 90° at the shoulder relative to the floor. The forearm lies flat, pointing forward.
  - The torso and legs form one straight line. Heels are slightly higher than the body would be if it were level, because of the toes. The body slopes very slightly upward from the feet to the shoulders: the shoulder is about one upper-arm length off the floor, the ankle about one foot length.
  - The neck continues the line of the spine. Gaze is at the floor just ahead of the hands.
- Hands: ACE shows palms down with the forearms parallel. That is clearer in a side view than clasped hands, and matches ACE. Recommend palms down, forearms parallel.
- Feet hip-width (from the brief) is not stated by ACE. ACE's plank wording does not set a foot width. Hip-width is a reasonable choice; it is not visible in a pure side view anyway.
- For a 5-second loop of a hold, the only motion should be breathing (a subtle rib or abdomen rise). The hips must not drift.
- **Three mistakes never to show:**
  1. Hips sagging so the low back arches toward the floor.
  2. Hips piked up into an inverted "V".
  3. Elbows ahead of the shoulders, or shoulders shrugged to the ears; head hanging or craned up.

### Gaps
- No reachable primary source specified foot width, or forearms parallel versus hands clasped. Both appear in practice. The FM 7-22 forearm plank text was not retrieved.

## 3. Pull-up (pronated grip, strict)

### Takeaway
Overhand grip, slightly wider than the shoulders. Start from a full dead hang with straight elbows. First depress the shoulder blades (shoulders pull down away from the ears) without bending the elbows. Then pull until the chin clears the bar, without craning the neck. Lower under control to straight arms. The legs stay quiet: no kip and no swing.

### Cited Findings
- Grip width: people freely choose a grip 20–50% wider than shoulder width. "Pull-up" is commonly tied to the overhand (pronated, palms facing away) grip (search-snippet of Wikipedia) — [Wikipedia: Pull-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pull-up)
- Test standard (IPFT flexed-arm hang): overhand grip, hands shoulder-width apart, chin level with the bar (snippet) — [Topend Sports IPFT](https://www.topendsports.com/testing/tests/flexed-arm-hang-ipft.htm)
- Scapular depression first: "start by depressing the scapulae first and keep the shoulders down as you pull". Do not over-extend the cervical spine to get the chin over the bar (NFPT, a trainer certification body; snippet) — [NFPT](https://www.nfpt.com/?p=10925)
- Top criterion: the chin passes the horizontal plane of the bar; touching with the nose or chest is not required (snippet) — [Bullbarfit](https://bullbarfit.com/blogs/q-as/how-to-measure-pull-up-form-and-correctness)
- Descent: "keep the negative slow and controlled ... all the way to the bottom of a dead hang — straight elbows and relaxed shoulders" (snippet) — [TrainHeroic](https://www.trainheroic.com/blog/strict-pull-ups)
- A hidden fault: you can get the chin over the bar with the shoulders shrugged (scapular elevation) (snippet) — [Range of Motion](https://rangeofmotion.net.au/why-you-should-add-strict-chest-to-bar-pull-ups-to-your-training/)
- A pronated grip shifts elbow-flexor work toward the brachialis and brachioradialis and limits the biceps' contribution — [HPRC](https://hprc-online.org/physical-fitness/training-performance/change-your-hand-position-optimize-your-workouts)

### Inferences
- **Side-view keyframes:**
  - **Bottom:** arms fully straight overhead, body hanging vertically below the bar. The knees may be slightly bent with the ankles crossed, or the legs may be straight together. Choose one and keep it fixed for the whole loop.
  - **About 0.3–0.5 s in:** a small scapular set. The body rises 2–5 cm with the elbows still straight, and the shoulders move away from the ears.
  - **Pull:** the elbows drive down and slightly back. The chest rises toward the bar, with a slight lean back of the torso so the face clears the bar.
  - **Top (about 2 s):** chin just above the bar, neck neutral, elbows fully bent and pointing down. The bar is at the level of the upper chest or collarbone, slightly in front of the face.
  - **Descent:** slower than the pull, back to the dead hang.
- Thumb: the conventional and safest depiction is a full grip, with the thumb wrapped around the bar opposite the fingers. **I found no citable source for this in the session.** Treat it as a convention, not a cited fact.
- **Three mistakes never to show:**
  1. Kipping, swinging or leg kicks (any hip drive).
  2. Partial range: starting with the elbows bent, or stopping short of the chin.
  3. Chin "reaching" (the neck craned up), or shoulders shrugged to the ears at the top.

### Gaps
- The FM 7-22/H2F pull-up text, ExRx, and the Royal Navy guidance were not retrieved (403 errors and search misses). No primary source was obtained on thumb position or exact grip width. The coaching sources here were mostly read at snippet level.

## 4. Bent-over barbell row (overhand)

### Takeaway
Hip-hinged stance with the knees softly bent and the spine neutral (flat back). Overhand grip just outside shoulder width. Arms hang straight with the bar about at the knees or upper shins. Row the bar to between the belly button and the sternum (lower ribs / upper abdomen). Elbows travel back at about 45° from the torso. The torso stays still and must not swing. For a general audience, recommend a torso angle of about 45° above horizontal rather than near-parallel. Lower-back load is the main risk.

### Cited Findings
- Onnit (coaching): feet hip-width apart; grip "just outside shoulder width", palms down. Hips go back with head, spine and pelvis aligned; knees bend naturally. Torso "just above parallel to the floor", or as low as possible while the lower back stays neutral. Shoulder blades back and down ("proud chest"). Neck neutral, eyes on the floor a few feet ahead — [Onnit Academy](https://onnit.com/academy/bent-over-row)
- Onnit: the bar starts with the arms hanging "touching your shins just below the knees" and is rowed to touch "between your belly button and sternum". At the top the upper arms "point out at about a 45-degree angle". Pause 1–2 s; lower under control — [Onnit Academy](https://onnit.com/academy/bent-over-row)
- Onnit mistakes: torso bouncing ("can injure the lower back"); elbows pulled too high (shoulder irritation); standing up mid-set and using momentum; rounding the lower back — [Onnit Academy](https://onnit.com/academy/bent-over-row)
- Baechle & Earle, *Weight Training: Steps to Success* (Earle and Baechle are also the editors of NSCA's Essentials): the start has the shoulders 10–30° higher than the hips (search-snippet level, not read in full) — search result for [Human Kinetics excerpt](https://canada.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/bent-over-row) (now redirects; unverified)
- Other coaching: aim for a torso near parallel with a flat back; rise higher if the hamstrings or low back cannot hold that (snippet) — [Fitwill](https://fitwill.app/exercise/8790/bodyweight-bent-over-row/)
- Peer-reviewed: among the inverted row, standing bent-over row and one-arm cable row, the bent-over row produced **the largest lumbar spine load** (and the highest spine stiffness). The authors suggest the inverted row for people who want to spare the low back. n = 7 healthy males — [Fenwick, Brown & McGill 2009, JSCR (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19197209/)
- Elbows tight to the body mostly use the lats and teres major; a high row with the elbows near shoulder height shifts work to the deltoids, rhomboids and mid-traps — [HPRC](https://hprc-online.org/physical-fitness/training-performance/change-your-hand-position-optimize-your-workouts)
- An attribution to NSCA that 45° is "a safer starting point for novices" appeared only on a low-quality page and could **not** be verified — [search result](https://library.peoplespharmacy.com/bent-rows-work) (unreliable)

### Inferences
- **Torso angle for a general-audience app:** the sources range from about 10–30° above the hips (Baechle & Earle, snippet) to "just above parallel" (Onnit), and some use about 45°. Given the lumbar load finding (Fenwick 2009), the safest depiction is a torso at **about 30–45° above horizontal** with a clearly flat back. Never show near-parallel with any rounding. This choice is my synthesis; no reachable primary source fixes the number.
- **Side-view keyframes:**
  - **Start/bottom:** feet hip-width apart, shins nearly vertical, knees bent about 15–25° (estimate). Hips pushed back. The back is flat from the pelvis to the head, with the head in line (gaze about 1–2 m ahead on the floor). Arms hang vertically from the shoulders; the bar sits at or just below the knees, over the mid-foot.
  - **Top (about 2 s):** the bar touches the lower ribs / upper abdomen. Elbows are behind the torso, at about 45° from the body, with the upper arm about level with the torso. Shoulder blades squeezed. **The torso angle is identical to the start frame.**
  - **Path:** the bar travels vertically, staying over the mid-foot. Only the arms and shoulder blades move.
- **Three mistakes never to show:**
  1. A rounded lower or upper back (a hunched spine).
  2. The torso swinging up as the bar rises, using momentum or standing up mid-rep.
  3. Elbows flared out to shoulder height, or the bar pulled to the chest or neck, or shoulders shrugged.

### Gaps
- NSCA's *Exercise Technique Manual* and *Essentials* text on the bent-over row was not retrieved, so an authoritative NSCA torso angle and the bar contact point are not confirmed. ExRx was blocked (403). The Baechle & Earle 10–30° figure is snippet-only.
- No source gave a numeric knee angle. The knee and shin values above are illustrative estimates.
