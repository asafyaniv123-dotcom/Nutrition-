## 1. Bodyweight Squat: stance, depth, knee tracking, heels, torso, arms, sequencing

### Takeaway
Feet slightly wider than hip width with toes turned slightly out. The hips go back and down while the knees bend at the same time. Go down to thighs parallel or nearly parallel, keeping the heels flat and the back flat. At the bottom, seen from the side, the shin and torso are roughly parallel. On the way up, the hips and torso rise together. Knees moving moderately forward past the toes is normal. Do not illustrate a "knees never pass the toes" squat.

### Cited Findings
- **Start:** feet slightly wider than hip width, toes turned slightly outward, arms hanging at the sides with palms facing in, shoulders pulled down and back. — [ACE, Bodyweight Squat](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/135/bodyweight-squat/)
- **Brace:** tighten the core, lift and open the chest, head tilted slightly up, weight shifted toward the heels while pushing the hips back "toward the wall behind you." — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/135/bodyweight-squat/)
- **Sequencing:** send the hips back and then down, with hips and knees bending together. The knees gradually move forward, but how far the shins travel should be controlled. Keep the back flat. — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/135/bodyweight-squat/)
- **Depth rule:** lower until the thighs are parallel or nearly parallel to the floor, *or* until the heels start to lift, *or* until the torso begins to round, whichever comes first. — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/135/bodyweight-squat/)
- **Knee tracking:** knees stay aligned over the second toe. Ankles do not collapse inward or outward, and the feet do not shift. — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/135/bodyweight-squat/)
- **Bottom, side view:** shins and torso roughly parallel. Weight is spread evenly between the balls of the feet and the heels. The lower back looks flat or is only just starting to round. — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/135/bodyweight-squat/)
- **Ascent:** keep the back, chest and head position. Exhale while extending the hips and knees and pressing through the heels. Hips and torso rise together, heels stay flat, knees stay over the second toe. Inhale on the way down. — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/135/bodyweight-squat/)
- **Knees past toes:** Fry, Smith & Schilling (2003, JSCR) used a barrier to stop the knees moving past the toes. Knee torque fell from 150.1 to 117.3 N·m, but hip torque rose from 28.2 to 302.7 N·m. They concluded that restricting the knees "may minimize stress on the knees" but that forces are likely transferred "inappropriately to the hips and low back." — [PubMed 14636100](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14636100/)
  - Caveats: the sample was small (7 recreationally trained men). A later study did not reproduce a tenfold rise in hip torque. The result concerns joint torque, not injury risk. — [Physio Network summary](https://www.physio-network.com/blog/knees-shouldnt-pass-toes-during-the-squat-myth-or-truth/) **(snippet)**; [ISBS/Konstanz proceedings](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/3248/3050) **(snippet)**

### Inferences
- **Arms:** ACE only gives the arms for the start position (at the sides). For a side-view loop the clearest choice is arms rising forward to about shoulder height as the hips descend, as a counterbalance, then returning. If the brief wants to follow ACE literally, keep the arms at the sides. Either is defensible; arms reaching forward reads better from the side. [illustrator default; no source found]
- **Angles at the bottom [illustrator default]:** knee about 90 to 100° (thigh horizontal), hip flexion about 90 to 100°, torso leaning forward about 40 to 45° from vertical and parallel to the shin, knee slightly ahead of the toes, whole sole on the floor.
- **Safest depth for a general audience:** "parallel or nearly parallel," with the ACE cut-offs (heels lift or back rounds). Do not show a deep, below-parallel squat.
- **Three things the demo must never show:** (1) heels lifting off the floor; (2) a rounding lower back or the "butt wink" pelvis tuck at the bottom; (3) knees collapsing inward, which is less visible from the side, so instead avoid knees drifting far past the toes while the hips stay forward, i.e. a knee-first squat with no hip hinge. All three come from ACE's cues above.

### Gaps
- No authoritative numeric value for torso angle or toe-out angle. Commonly cited figures are around 15 to 30° toe-out, but I found no fetched source for them.

## 2. Bodyweight Forward Lunge (step forward, return to standing)

### Takeaway
Stand with feet together or hip-width apart. Take a long step forward with ONE foot, while the back foot stays planted on the ball of the foot. Lower straight down until both knees are about 90°, with the front shin roughly vertical, the front knee over the ankle and not past the toes, the back knee hovering just above the floor, and the trunk upright. Push through the front heel to step back to the start.

### Cited Findings
- **Sequence:** "Take a long step forward so that most of your weight is on your front leg. Bend your front knee so that it is at a right angle. Your back knee should also be at a right angle. Keep your trunk upright and don't let your front knee go over your toes. Push up through the heel of your front leg, and step back so your feet are together again." — [Humber Teaching NHS Foundation Trust, Lower limb strengthening (Intermediate), physiotherapy handout](https://www.humber.nhs.uk/downloads/Physiotherapy%20downloads/Lower%20limb%20strengthening%20Intermediate.pdf) **(snippet; the PDF returned 404 when fetched)**
- **Lead leg alignment:** "Even a slight turn of the lead foot or lead knee inward during a forward lunge can increase the torque stress of the knee joint of the front leg." — [NSCA Personal Training Quarterly 4.4, "The Undervalued Lunge"](https://dxpprod.nsca.com/contentassets/24dd7222ed1b4caeb8a0a46b81bd11f3/ptq-4.4.9-the-undervalued-lunge.pdf) **(snippet; 403 when fetched)**
- **Back knee:** sources disagree. Some say "nearly touches the floor" — [Scribd L2 Lunges handout](https://www.scribd.com/document/663749339/L2-Lunges) **(snippet)**; [Runner's World UK, split squat](https://www.pressreader.com/uk/runners-world-uk/20200701/282986812155314) **(snippet)**. Others allow a light tap or say to hover with no contact (various low-authority guides; see the search note in Gaps).
- **ACE equivalent (side lunge, same principles):** the stepping leg's shin ends vertical with the knee over the second toe, heels stay flat, and you push off the stepping leg to return. Stepping too far makes it hard to keep the shin over the foot. "Take a shorter step." — [ACE, Side Lunge](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/50/glute-bridge/) (the URL slug says glute-bridge, but the page served is ACE's Side Lunge)

### Inferences
- **Which foot moves:** only the front foot steps out and back. The back foot stays on its original spot the whole time, with its heel lifting so the back foot pivots onto the ball of the foot and toes as the body lowers. This matches the NHS handout ("step back so your feet are together again").
- **Step length [illustrator default]:** long enough that both knees reach about 90° with the front shin vertical. In practice this is roughly 2 to 2.5 foot lengths, or about leg length × 0.8.
- **Bottom position, side view [illustrator default]:** front thigh horizontal; front shin vertical, knee directly above the ankle; back thigh vertical, pointing down from the hip; back shin roughly horizontal, with the knee about 2 to 5 cm above the floor; torso vertical, with hips directly between the feet; front foot fully flat. Body weight goes straight down between the feet, not forward onto the front knee.
- **Path:** step forward, then lower vertically (the hips drop straight down), then push off through the front heel, which brings the front foot back beside the back foot. In a 5-second loop: about 1 s for the step, 1.5 s lowering, a short hold at the bottom, then about 1.5 s returning.
- **Safest version for a general audience:** a hover (no knee contact), front knee over the ankle and not past the toes (Humber NHS), trunk upright.
- **Three things the demo must never show:** (1) front knee traveling past the toes, or the front heel lifting; (2) the back knee slamming into the floor; (3) the torso leaning forward over the front thigh. From the side it is also worth avoiding a step that is too short, which forces (1).

### Gaps
- I could not fetch a primary ACE forward-lunge page (wrong URL) or the full NSCA PTQ article. No authoritative numeric step length was found. Search notes on back-knee clearance (e.g. "2 to 4 inches") came from low-authority sites and are not used.

## 3. Conventional Barbell Deadlift

### Takeaway
Feet about hip width under the bar, with the bar over the midfoot, almost touching the shins. Grip just outside the legs (double overhand in the demo). Shoulders slightly in front of the bar, neutral back. The hips and shoulders rise at the same rate, and the bar travels in a vertical line over the midfoot, staying close to the legs. Lock out with the hips and knees fully extended, standing tall without leaning back. To lower, the hips go back first, then the knees bend once the bar passes them.

### Cited Findings
- **Setup (University of Nebraska Medical Center, Center for Healthy Living video transcript):** feet under the hips; bar just in front of the shins, over the midfoot; hands just outside the hips, mixed or pronated grip, thumbs wrapped; shoulders slightly in front of the bar. — [UNMC deadlift transcript](https://www.unmc.edu/cfhl/_documents/deadlift_video_transcript.txt)
- **Lift:** heels down throughout; the bar moves straight up over the middle of the foot; keep the lumbar curve; the hips and shoulders rise at the same rate; shoulders down and back, torso braced. — [UNMC](https://www.unmc.edu/cfhl/_documents/deadlift_video_transcript.txt)
- **Lockout:** full hip and knee extension. — [UNMC](https://www.unmc.edu/cfhl/_documents/deadlift_video_transcript.txt)
- **Lowering:** "hips back first, then down," keeping the bar over the midfoot. — [UNMC](https://www.unmc.edu/cfhl/_documents/deadlift_video_transcript.txt)
- **Bar path:** the bar is most efficiently pulled in an essentially vertical line over the middle of the foot, with the shoulders just in front of the bar. — [Starting Strength](https://startingstrength.com/training/the-primary-start-position-error-in-the-deadlift) **(snippet)**
- **Shoulder blades:** the scapulae sit directly over the bar at the start (the shoulders themselves are slightly in front). — [Powerlifting to Win, Deadlift Setup Science](https://www.powerliftingtowin.com/powerlifting-technique-deadlift-setup/) **(snippet)**
- **Setup errors:** dropping the hips too low can push the bar forward of the midfoot. — [Powerlifting to Win](https://www.powerliftingtowin.com/powerlifting-technique-deadlift-setup/) **(snippet)**
- **Lockout error:** squeeze the glutes and stand tall, avoiding excessive backward lean or hyperextending the lower back. — [Breaking Muscle, 3 most common deadlift errors](https://breakingmuscle.com/the-3-most-common-deadlift-errors-and-how-to-fix-them/) **(snippet)**
- **Back rounding** is the most common deadlift error. — [Jeff Nippard, 4 most common deadlift errors](https://rosetta.to/u/jeffnippard/the-4-most-common-deadlift-errors-how-to-fix-them) **(snippet)**
- **Bar drift:** the bar drifting away from the body is caused by starting too far from the bar, the shoulders too far forward, or not enough lat tension. Keep the bar in contact with, or close to, the legs. — [Barbell Logic, fixing common deadlift mistakes](https://barbell-logic.com/fixing-common-deadlift-mistakes) **(snippet)**

### Inferences
- **Start position, side view [illustrator default]:** the bar's vertical line passes through the midfoot (roughly the instep / the middle of the laces), about 2 to 3 cm in front of the shin. Shins angle forward until they touch or nearly touch the bar. Hips are higher than the knees and lower than the shoulders. Torso about 30 to 45° above horizontal, back straight (a straight line from the neck to the pelvis). Arms vertical and straight, hanging from shoulders that sit slightly ahead of the bar. Head neutral, gaze a little ahead on the floor.
- **Path:** the bar moves strictly vertically. As it passes the knees, the knees move back out of its way and the hips push forward. Lockout means standing tall: ear, shoulder, hip, knee and ankle stacked on one vertical line, bar resting against the upper thighs, arms straight. The lowering mirrors the lift: hips back first with knees nearly straight until the bar passes the knees, then the knees bend.
- **Three things the demo must never show:** (1) a rounded (flexed) back at any point; (2) leaning back or hyperextending at lockout, or a shrug-and-lean finish; (3) the bar drifting forward away from the legs, or hips shooting up first so the lift becomes a stiff-legged pull.
- **Grip:** double overhand (pronated) is the right choice for a demo. UNMC lists it as an option, and it reads symmetrically from the side. Hands "just outside the hips" (UNMC) is equivalent to "just outside the legs" in the brief.

### Gaps
- The often-quoted "bar about 1 inch from the shins" and NSCA's exact deadlift wording were not verified. The NSCA *Exercise Technique Manual for Resistance Training* (4th ed., [Human Kinetics](https://us.humankinetics.com/products/exercise-technique-manual-for-resistance-training-4th-edition-epub)) has a deadlift entry, but I could not access it. ExRx (403) could not be read.

## 4. Glute Bridge (bodyweight, on the floor)

### Takeaway
Lie on your back with knees bent, feet flat and hip-width apart, toes pointing forward. Heels are about 6 to 8 in (15 to 20 cm) from the glutes, or close enough that your fingertips touch your heels. Arms rest on the floor at your sides. Brace, tuck the pelvis slightly, and drive the hips up with the glutes until the shoulders, hips and knees form one straight line. Then lower with control. Lift with the hips, not the back: no arching above that line.

### Cited Findings
- **Setup:** heels "approximately 6 to 8 inches from the glutes," feet hip-width apart, toes straight ahead, arms resting at the sides with palms up. — [NASM, Why the glute bridge belongs in almost every client program](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/blog/training/why-the-glute-bridge-belongs-in-almost-every-client-program)
- **Top:** lift the hips until there is "a straight line from the knees to the shoulders," pause briefly. Brace the core first, keep a neutral spine, slightly tuck the pelvis, squeeze the glutes. — [NASM](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/blog/training/why-the-glute-bridge-belongs-in-almost-every-client-program)
- **Compensations:** excessive spinal extension is a compensation, so cue "lift with your hips, not your back." Feeling it in the hamstrings means the feet are too far away; feeling it in the low back means too much extension or not enough core. — [NASM](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/blog/training/why-the-glute-bridge-belongs-in-almost-every-client-program)
- **Foot and knee spacing:** keep hip-width distance between the knees and the feet; top = a straight line from shoulders to knees; neutral spine. — [Physitrack, glute bridge](https://www.physitrack.com/exercise-library/how-to-perform-the-glute-bridge-exercise)
- **Pelvis tuck, then lift:** "Tuck the pelvis in…push your hips towards the ceiling…until your body forms a straight line from head to knees," squeeze at the top, lower with control. — [StrengthLog, Glute Bridge](https://www.strengthlog.com/glute-bridge/)
- **Fingertip check:** fingertips should be able to touch the heels. — [The Gym Group, how to do a glute bridge](https://www.thegymgroup.com/exercises/legs-and-glutes-exercises/how-to-do-a-glute-bridge) **(snippet)**
- **Over-arching:** over-arching at the top is a common mistake; keep "ribs down and stacked." — [Vitality, glute bridge cues](https://shopvitality.com/blogs/vitality-fitness/how-to-do-glute-bridge) **(snippet)**

### Inferences
- **Start, side view [illustrator default]:** head, upper back and pelvis on the floor. Knee angle about 75 to 80° (heels 15 to 20 cm from the glutes). Arms straight along the floor beside the torso.
- **Top, side view [illustrator default]:** only the upper back and shoulder blades, head and arms touch the floor, plus the soles of the feet. Knee angle about 90° with the shins roughly vertical, which follows from the 6 to 8 in heel distance once the hips rise. The line from shoulder to hip to knee is straight (about 0° at the hip): no sag and no lift beyond it. Ribs stay down. Feet flat, with neither the heels nor the toes lifting. The hips rise along an arc around the feet, moving up and slightly forward.
- **Three things the demo must never show:** (1) lumbar hyperextension at the top, meaning the hips pushed above the shoulder-knee line or the ribs flaring; (2) feet far from the hips, so the legs are nearly straight (the hamstring version) or heels/toes lifting off the floor; (3) the hips sagging at the top, never reaching the straight line, or the lift coming from pushing up through the neck and head.

### Gaps
- No primary source gives a numeric knee angle at the top. 90° is an inference from the NASM heel distance plus the common cue that the shins end vertical. The Hinge Health bridge guide was in the results but its steps were not retrieved.

## 5. Where sources disagree, and the safest choice for a general-audience app

### Takeaway
Depth for the squat: parallel or nearly parallel, stopping earlier if the heels lift or the back rounds. Knees past toes: in the squat, allow moderate forward travel; in the lunge, show the front knee over the ankle. Back knee in the lunge: hover, never touch.

### Cited Findings
- Squat depth: ACE uses "parallel or nearly parallel," with stop rules for heels lifting or the back rounding — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/135/bodyweight-squat/).
- Knees past toes in the squat: blocking forward knee travel moves load to the hips and low back (Fry 2003) — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14636100/). ACE also lets the knees move forward, but in a controlled way — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/135/bodyweight-squat/).
- Knees past toes in the lunge: an NHS physiotherapy handout says don't let the front knee go over the toes — [Humber NHS](https://www.humber.nhs.uk/downloads/Physiotherapy%20downloads/Lower%20limb%20strengthening%20Intermediate.pdf) **(snippet)**.
- Back knee in the lunge: some say "nearly touches the floor," others allow a brief tap (see section 2).
- Deadlift grip: UNMC allows mixed or pronated — [UNMC](https://www.unmc.edu/cfhl/_documents/deadlift_video_transcript.txt).

### Inferences
- These choices do not conflict. In the squat, the knee moving slightly ahead of the toes is fine and expected. In the lunge, a correct step length puts the front knee over the ankle anyway. In both cases the animation should simply never show an extreme.
- The deadlift is the one exercise in this set with a load. A bodyweight version might be better for a general-audience app, but that is a product decision.

### Gaps
- No single governing body (ACSM/NSCA) source covering all four movements could be read in full during this session.
