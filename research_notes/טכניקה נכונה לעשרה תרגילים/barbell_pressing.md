# Barbell pressing technique for a side-view 5-second loop: flat Barbell Bench Press (priority) and standing Barbell Overhead Press

Source-quality note for the writer: I opened the Noteboom et al. 2024 paper (PMC), the ACE "Chest Press" page, an NSCA-CPT-aligned study guide (open-exam-prep, secondary, not NSCA itself), the Juggernaut (JTS) help-centre page on gripping the bench bar, the CrossFit "Shoulder Press" essentials page, and Barbell Logic's overhead press mistakes page. These pages could not be opened: ExRx (403), powerliftingtechnique.com (403) and JMU's NSCA-based KIN 425 page (DNS failure). Findings from those three come from search-engine summaries and are labelled that way. **I could not open the NSCA Exercise Technique Manual itself**: it is a paid Human Kinetics book, and the only copies online are unverified PDFs on unrelated domains. So the NSCA's exact wording ("closed, pronated grip", "slightly wider than shoulder width") is **not verified** here.

## Bench press — grip width

### Takeaway
For a general audience, use a grip "slightly wider than shoulder width", with the hands at **no more than about 1.5x biacromial (shoulder-tip to shoulder-tip) width**. The tested range is 1 to 2 BAW. At the bottom, the forearms should look vertical from the front. Wide grips raise acromioclavicular (AC) and glenohumeral (GH) joint loads. Sources do not conflict on safety: every one points to moderate width, not wide.

### Cited Findings
- ACE (beginner level): "Lie face up on a flat bench, and grip a barbell with the hands slightly wider than shoulder-width." — [ACE Chest Press](https://acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/5/chest-press)
- Noteboom et al. 2024 tested grips of 1, 1.5 and 2 bi-acromial widths (BAW), each marked with tape on the bar from the lifter's measured biacromial width. They also tested shoulder abduction of 45°, 70° and 90°, set with a goniometer. — [Noteboom et al. 2024, Front Physiol (PMC11224528)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11224528/)
- Same paper: "narrower grip widths of <1.5 BAW decreased acromioclavicular compression". Wider grips increased GH total force, compression and posterior shear, and the largest jump came from 1.5 to 2 BAW. Supraspinatus and subscapularis activity was lower with smaller grips. The authors recommend a grip below 1.5 BAW combined with scapular retraction because this "may decrease the risk for certain bench press injuries", and they note that "wide grips may increase the injury risk". — [Noteboom et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11224528/)
- Caveats from the same paper: n=10, a light 16 kg bar, and modelled loads rather than injury outcomes. A 1 BAW grip *increased* GH superior shear compared with 1.5 BAW. For lifters with pain above the GH joint, the authors suggest it "might be better to avoid ... 45° and narrow grip widths of 1 BAW". The six unsafe combinations, such as 90° abduction with 1 BAW, were excluded. — [Noteboom et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11224528/)
- Injury context from the same paper: distal clavicular osteolysis is estimated at 27% prevalence in competitive weightlifters, and pectoralis major rupture at about 6 per 1,000 soldiers per year, almost all from bench pressing. — [Noteboom et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11224528/)
- Green & Comfort 2007 (Strength Cond J), via a search summary of the Salford repository record: grips wider than 1.5 biacromial width may increase the risk of anterior shoulder instability, distal clavicle osteolysis and pec major rupture. Reducing the grip to 1.5 BAW or less changes 1RM by about ±5% and does not change muscle recruitment. The claims were largely expert opinion and theory. — [Salford repository: Green & Comfort](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1804489/the-affect-of-grip-width-on-bench-press-performance-and-risk-of-injury)
- JTS gives no number. It defines width by alignment instead: the elbows should "align naturally under the bar", with the wrist "directly over your elbow" at the chest. — [JTS: Bench Pillar 3, Gripping the Bar](https://help.jtsstrength.com/en/articles/69-bench-pillar-3-gripping-the-bar)
- The competition upper limit, for reference only: under IPF rules the index fingers must be within the **81 cm marks** (the rings on a power bar). Source: the 2026 IPF referee briefing, read through a search summary because the full rulebook PDF was not opened. — [IPF Referee Briefing 2026 (FFForce copy)](https://www.ffforce.fr/medias/fichiers/Saison%202026/Force%20Athletique/Reglement/IPF%20Referee%20Briefing%202026%20-%20DC%20seul_FR(1).pdf); [2026 IPF Technical Rulebook](https://www.irishpowerliftingfederation.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2026-IPF-Technical-Rulebook-effective-01-March-2026-v3.pdf)

### Inferences
- Illustrator rule: the hand-to-hand distance is about **1.3 to 1.5x the distance between the two shoulder tips (acromions)**. Seen from the front at the bottom of the rep, the forearms look vertical. The 81 cm rings are a maximum, not a target, so a general-audience demo should keep each hand clearly inside its ring.
- Because the loop is side-on, width barely shows. It does show through two cues: the forearm stays vertical in the side view, and the far hand is hidden behind the near one.

### Gaps
- The NSCA's own wording on grip width could not be verified because the primary text was inaccessible.
- No prospective injury study validates the 1.5 BAW threshold. It rests on modelling and expert opinion.

## Bench press — the bar in the hand, wrist, thumbs, knuckles

### Takeaway
Use a **closed grip with the thumbs wrapped around the bar**. The bar sits **low in the palm, on the heel of the hand near the base of the thumb**, directly over the forearm bones, not up in the fingers. The wrist is stacked over the forearm: neutral, or bent back only very slightly. The thumbless "suicide" grip is a safety fault.

### Cited Findings
- The bar should sit "low in the palm, near the base of the thumb, to align properly with the forearm". "A slight backward bend in the wrist is acceptable, allowing the bar to sit securely over the forearm." Two faults: a wrist so straight that the bar sits too far forward in the hand, and a wrist bent back too far. — [JTS](https://help.jtsstrength.com/en/articles/69-bench-pillar-3-gripping-the-bar)
- Use a closed grip with the thumb wrapped; "a thumbless grip is a fault when the question is safety"; "Wrist stacked over the forearm, not cocked back." — [NSCA-CPT study guide (secondary)](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/nsca-cpt/resistance-technique/press-pull-technique)
- Search summaries of coaching sources agree that a bar held in the fingers levers the wrist into extension (pain, poor force transfer). Some allow a slight backward bend as long as the bar stays over the wrist joint; others call for a straight line. — [powerliftingtechnique.com (search summary; page 403)](https://powerliftingtechnique.com/bench-press-wrist-position/); [Torokhtiy guide](https://store.torokhtiy.com/blogs/guides/bench-press-wrist-position)
- In the thumbless grip the thumb sits on the same side as the fingers. If the bar slips, nothing stops it rolling onto the chest, neck or face. Powerlifting competition bans it and requires the thumbs around the bar. — [Fitness Volt: Suicide Grip](https://fitnessvolt.com/suicide-grip/); [Lift Vault](https://liftvault.com/resources/suicide-grip/); IPF thumbs-around rule via [IPF Referee Briefing 2026](https://www.ffforce.fr/medias/fichiers/Saison%202026/Force%20Athletique/Reglement/IPF%20Referee%20Briefing%202026%20-%20DC%20seul_FR(1).pdf)
- The JTS fix for early elbow flare is "squeezing the bar harder with your pinky". — [JTS](https://help.jtsstrength.com/en/articles/69-bench-pillar-3-gripping-the-bar)

### Inferences (illustrator spec, side view)
- **Pronated grip**: palms face the feet (away from the face), the knuckles point up toward the ceiling and slightly back toward the head, and the fingers wrap over the bar toward the lifter.
- **Bar in the hand**: the bar crosses the palm diagonally, sitting in the fleshy heel at the base of the thumb. The wrap order is thumb under and around the bar from the head side, then fingers over the top, closing on the thumb. A vertical line through the bar should pass down through the wrist into the forearm.
- **Wrist**: the back of the hand continues the line of the forearm, with at most a small backward tilt of about 10-20° (my estimate; no source gives a number). The "looks weird" failure is very likely a hand bent back about 45-90° with the bar in the fingers, or thumbs beside the fingers.
- Two things the demo must never show: fingers holding the bar with the palm open, and thumbs on top of the bar.

### Gaps
- No primary source gives a numeric limit for acceptable wrist extension; "slight" is the most precise wording found.
- No source I opened describes knuckle orientation explicitly. The description above is derived from what a pronated grip means.

## Bench press — forearms, elbows, touch point, bar path, start and lockout

### Takeaway
Start and finish with **straight arms and the bar over the shoulder joint**. Lower under control to **the lower chest, around the nipple line or lower sternum**, then press back up and slightly toward the head (a shallow J-curve). At the bottom, from the side, the **forearm is vertical and the wrist is stacked over the elbow**. The upper arm sits about **45-75° from the torso**. Flaring to 90° is a fault.

### Cited Findings
- Unrack to straight arms with the bar over the chest and shoulders. Touch the "lower pec / nipple line, not the neck". "Touch the chest with control." Keep the elbows about 45-75° from the torso, because a "90-degree flare dumps stress into the anterior shoulder". "Do not bounce the bar off the chest." — [NSCA-CPT study guide (secondary)](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/nsca-cpt/resistance-technique/press-pull-technique)
- With the bar on the chest it should be "stacked directly over your wrist" and "the wrist should be directly over your elbow". Over-tucking and over-flaring are both faults. — [JTS](https://help.jtsstrength.com/en/articles/69-bench-pillar-3-gripping-the-bar)
- ACE: "Slowly lower the bar to the chest by allowing the elbows to bend out to the side", "Stop when the elbows are just below the bench", "press the weight straight up". — [ACE Chest Press](https://acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/5/chest-press)
- Noteboom: a 45° abduction angle increased GH superior shear compared with 70°, larger angles increased GH compression over short phases, and pec major activity was lowest at small abduction angles. The 90° and 1 BAW combination was excluded as unsafe. — [Noteboom et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11224528/)
- Bar path, from search summaries of coaching sources: a J-curve that starts over the shoulders and descends to a point between the nipple line and the bottom of the sternum. A narrower grip touches lower. Stronger by Science reports that improving elite lifters pushed the bar back toward the face on the way up. — [Power Athlete HQ](https://powerathletehq.com/the-bench-press-j-curve-or-vertical-bar-path/); [Stronger by Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/bench-press-bar-path/); [powerliftingtechnique.com](https://powerliftingtechnique.com/best-bench-press-bar-path/)

### Inferences (side-view keyframes for a 5 s loop)
1. **0.0 s, lockout**: arms straight and vertical, the bar directly above the shoulder joint, roughly above the upper chest and collarbone line.
2. **0.0-2.0 s, descent**: the bar travels down and slightly toward the feet in a smooth arc. The elbows bend and drop below the bar while the forearms stay vertical.
3. **About 2.0-2.3 s, bottom**: the bar lightly touches the lower chest at the nipple line or lower sternum, with no bounce. From the side the forearm is vertical, with the wrist directly above the elbow, and the elbow sits slightly below the bench-top line. Seen from above, the upper arm is about 45-75° from the torso (60° is a safe middle value). In the side view this shows as the elbow sitting toward the feet from the shoulder, not level with it.
4. **2.3-4.5 s, press**: the bar goes up and slightly back toward the face, finishing over the shoulders again (the J).
5. **4.5-5.0 s**: hold at lockout so the loop closes on an identical frame.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study I opened directly measures the touch point or J-curve in recreational lifters. Bar path rests on coaching sources and on search summaries of the literature, such as Elliott et al. 1989.

## Bench press — setup: five points of contact, scapular retraction, eye position

### Takeaway
There are five points of contact: **head, upper back and shoulders, buttocks on the bench, and both feet flat on the floor**. The shoulder blades are pulled together and down, which gives a slight arch. At setup the **eyes are roughly under the bar**.

### Cited Findings
- "Set five points of contact: head, shoulders/upper back, buttocks, left foot, right foot." "Eyes start about under the bar." Losing five-point contact, or the buttocks leaving the bench, is listed as an error. — [NSCA-CPT study guide (secondary)](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/nsca-cpt/resistance-technique/press-pull-technique)
- JMU's NSCA-based KIN 425 materials list the same five points and say the position promotes "maximal stability and spinal support". This comes from a search summary; the page could not be opened. — [JMU KIN 425 ch.17](https://educ.jmu.edu/~strength/KIN_425/kin_425_chapter17_ex_tech_spotting.htm)
- Retraction was defined as squeezing the shoulder blades together and slightly arching the back. It lowered peak rotator-cuff activity in every condition and lowered AC and GH compression and posterior shear near the bottom. — [Noteboom et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11224528/)
- ACE: "Press the feet into the ground and the hips into the bench while lifting the bar off the rack." — [ACE Chest Press](https://acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/5/chest-press)

### Inferences
- Side view: the back of the head rests on the bench and the chin is neutral, neither lifted nor tucked hard. The shoulder blades are pinned to the bench and the chest is lifted. The lower back has a small natural arch, with a hand's-width gap at most; a general-audience demo should not show a competition bridge. The buttocks stay down for the whole rep. The feet are flat, with knees at about 90° or a little more and feet under or slightly behind the knees. The rack uprights sit above the eyes.

### Gaps
- No source I opened gives a numeric foot or knee angle or an arch height for general-population lifters.

## Bench press — three mistakes the demo must never show

### Takeaway
1. A **thumbless grip, or the bar held in the fingers with the wrist bent back**. This is the "weird grip" failure.
2. **Elbows flared to 90°**, with the bar coming down toward the neck or upper chest.
3. **Bouncing the bar off the chest, or the hips lifting off the bench**, which breaks the five points of contact.

### Cited Findings
- Faults listed: thumbless grip, wrist cocked back, 90° flare, bouncing, buttocks leaving the bench, losing five-point contact, lowering to the neck. — [NSCA-CPT study guide (secondary)](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/nsca-cpt/resistance-technique/press-pull-technique)
- Faults listed: overly straight or overly bent wrists, and over-tucking or over-flaring the elbows. — [JTS](https://help.jtsstrength.com/en/articles/69-bench-pillar-3-gripping-the-bar)
- The suicide grip risks the bar rolling onto the chest, neck or face. — [Fitness Volt](https://fitnessvolt.com/suicide-grip/)
- A wide grip above 1.5 BAW raises shoulder joint loads. — [Noteboom et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11224528/)

### Inferences
- A fourth mistake to avoid in the art: a very wide grip with hands at or beyond the rings, which shows as forearms angled outward at the bottom.

### Gaps
- None beyond the ones listed above.

## Overhead press (standing, barbell) — start, bar path, lockout, stance, lumbar, mistakes

### Takeaway
**Start**: stand hip-width with knees and hips locked. The bar rests high on the front delts and collarbones. The grip is closed, with hands just outside the shoulders and the bar in the heel of the palm. Wrists are straight and forearms vertical, with the elbows slightly in front of the bar. **Path**: the chin pulls back, the bar travels in a straight vertical line close to the face, and once it passes the forehead the head moves forward "through the window". **Lockout**: elbows locked, shoulders shrugged toward the ears, and the bar over the back of the head and ears, stacked over the shoulder joint and the midfoot. **Trunk**: glutes and abs braced, ribs down, no backward lean.

### Cited Findings
- CrossFit Shoulder Press: feet "approximately hip-width apart", the bar "resting on the shoulders", "the hands just outside the shoulders", elbows "pointing down and out and slightly in front of the bar". The bar travels "in a straight line to the overhead position", and the lifter must "pull the chin back at the onset". The rep is complete "when the arms are locked and the bar is over the ankles", while "actively pushing the shoulders towards the bar". The trunk is braced to prevent "undue hyperextension of the spine". Faults: leaning back or overextending, a forward arc, finishing forward of the ankles, and inactive shoulders or bent arms. — [CrossFit: The Shoulder Press](https://www.crossfit.com/essentials/the-shoulder-press)
- Barbell Logic: set the grip so the forearms are vertical (too wide is a common error). The bar goes "deep in the heel of the palm", with straight wrists and elbows up and in front of the bar. The bar moves vertically "directly over your midfoot". At lockout, shrug so "your shoulders close to your ears". Don't press forward, and don't press too far back. Note: the article's hip-pop and bounce cues belong to Barbell Logic's own style and should be omitted for a general audience. — [Barbell Logic: OHP Common Mistakes](https://barbell-logic.com/overhead-press-common-mistakes/)
- NSCA-aligned study guide: start at the "anterior deltoids / upper chest, wrists stacked, ribs down", glutes and abs braced. "Press the bar in a close path past the face", then "move the head slightly through so the finish is over the midfoot and the ears". Faults: "Lumbar hyperextension and a rib flare", the bar finishing in front of the face, and a hip dip, which turns the lift into a push press. — [NSCA-CPT study guide (secondary)](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/nsca-cpt/resistance-technique/press-pull-technique)
- Starting Strength (Rippetoe), via a search summary of an unofficial book excerpt and forum posts: the bar sits on the heel of the palms, and the elbows are just in front of the bar from the side, so the radius is vertical. At lockout the bar, the shoulder joint and the midfoot line up vertically, the elbows are locked, the traps are shrugged, and the bar is over the ears. — [Starting Strength forum: press grip width](https://startingstrength.com/resources/forum/technique/98074-press-grip-width.html); [Starting Strength: A Better Down, A Better Up](https://startingstrength.com/article/a-better-down-a-better-up)
- Dissent, via search summaries: RXMuscle cues bringing the bar back over the head with a neutral spine rather than pushing the head through. A StrongFirst post warns against pushing the head through too early. — [RXMuscle](https://www.rxmuscle.com/strength-articles/10988-fundamentals-of-overhead-pressing.html)

### Inferences (side-view keyframes, 5 s)
1. **0.0 s, rack**: standing tall. The bar sits on the front delts just under the chin, the forearms are vertical (the elbow slightly forward of the bar, under or a little ahead of it), the chin is tucked, the torso is vertical, and the knees are locked.
2. **0.0-1.0 s**: the head moves slightly back (chin back, not tilted up) while the bar rises vertically, nearly grazing the chin and nose.
3. **About 1.0-1.5 s**: the bar passes the forehead and the head moves forward under it "through the window" between the arms.
4. **About 2.0 s, lockout**: arms locked vertical, shoulders shrugged up to the ears, and the bar over the back of the head and ears, with the bar, shoulder joint, hip and midfoot on one vertical line. The ribs stay down and the lower back is not over-arched.
5. **2.0-5.0 s**: the head moves back again as the bar descends in the same straight line to the front delts, then a brief hold so the loop closes.
- **Three mistakes never to show**: (1) **leaning back** with an over-arched lower back and flared ribs; (2) **the bar arcing forward**, finishing in front of the face or forward of the feet, often with elbows behind the bar at the start; (3) **a knee or hip dip**, which makes it a push press, or bent arms at the top. Also avoid a grip so wide that the forearms angle outward, and wrists bent back with the bar in the fingers.

### Gaps
- No source I opened gives a numeric OHP grip width relative to biacromial width. "Just outside the shoulders" so that the forearms are vertical is as precise as it gets.
- No NSCA primary text was available for the shoulder press.
- Stance: CrossFit says hip-width. Other sources give no number.
