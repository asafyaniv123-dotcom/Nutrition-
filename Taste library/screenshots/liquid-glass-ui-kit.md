# liquid-glass-ui-kit.jpg

A component sheet — buttons, toggles, checkboxes, text fields, a card — in
translucent pastel glass on a flat light-grey ground.

## What Asaf said

> כאן אהבתי את הלחצנים שהם נראים כאילו כמו זכוכית עדינה ונקייה. חשוב באפליקציה
> שיש בה כל כך הרבה אזורים שתהיה מסודרת ונקייה ותתן חוויית משתמש לא מעמיסה.

Two things, and the second is the larger one.

**The buttons.** Delicate, clean glass. Not heavy, not loud.

**The reason.** This app has a lot of areas — nine on the home screen, six inside
fitness alone, three horizons in planning. With that much surface, the interface
has to stay orderly and quiet or it starts to weigh on the person using it. The
glass is a means to that; *not overloading* is the actual requirement.

## What is worth noticing in the sheet itself

The ground is flat and almost colourless, and every colour in the image sits
inside a control. Nothing is tinted except the thing you can press.

Each control is one soft, evenly-rounded shape with a light edge — depth comes
from that edge and a faint inner glow, not from a drop shadow underneath. The
colours are washes, never fills: you read the label through the colour rather
than against it.

The pastels are doing work, not decoration — blue, green, peach and pink each
mark a different kind of control, which is close to how this app already uses
one accent per area.

## Where it might apply

The area cards and the panel headers, most obviously — those are the surfaces
that repeat most and so set the weight of the whole app.

Worth saying out loud before anyone builds it: the app's palette is already
warm — cream ground, violet accent — and this sheet is cool and near-white. The
resemblance to reach for is the *lightness and the edge*, not the hue. Since
[[token-passes-not-redesigns]] the whole palette is one block of 36 tokens, so
that is a decision that can be made late and cheaply rather than assumed now.

## The risk to be honest about

Glass over a busy ground turns to mud. This app has a drifting sky behind the
home screen, photographs in the journal and the summaries, and coloured task
chips in the planner. Translucency reads as clean over the flat grey in this
image; over a photograph it reads as smeared. Whatever is built from this needs
trying on the busiest screen first, not the emptiest.
