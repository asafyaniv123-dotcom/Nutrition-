## What to notice

**ONE MATERIAL, TWO DIRECTIONS.** Nothing here is a different colour from
anything else. The whole screen is one white surface, and every element is
that surface pushed out or pressed in. A card is not "a white card on grey" —
it is the ground itself raised. That is what gives the depth, and it is why
the screen reads as calm rather than busy.

**THE COLOUR IS LIGHT, NOT PAINT.** The temperature ring is the only saturated
thing, and it behaves like a light source: it glows along its own edge and
tints the white beside it. Nothing else is coloured, so that one ring carries
the whole screen. This is the same idea as [[lit-when-done]], but the light is
attached to a value rather than to a state.

**THE SHADOWS ARE LONG AND ALMOST INVISIBLE.** Two of them on every element,
one light from the top-left, one dark from the bottom-right, both very wide
and very faint. Contrast between the lit and shaded edge is a few percent, not
twenty. That is the difference between this and the plastic version of the
same style.

**TYPE STAYS QUIET.** Thin grey labels, one weight, no boxes around them. The
form does the work.

## Where it applies

**The nutrition tubes.** A tube pressed into the ground with its level glowing
through is exactly this vocabulary, and it keeps the value as the only colour
on the screen.

## The hard part

**A glow needs a ground it can tint.** On a pure white page this reads as
premium; on a photograph or a strong gradient it turns muddy.

**And the contrast has to stay honest.** Soft grey on soft grey is where this
style fails accessibility. The numbers and labels must keep real contrast even
while the surfaces stay quiet - which is the objection that already exists
against `--muted` in the app today.
