# liquid-glass-panel.jpg

A whole panel, not a component sheet: one large translucent card floating over a
blurred photograph, with the controls from
[[liquid-glass-ui-kit]] arranged inside it.

Two separate judgements, and it is worth keeping them apart.

**Keep:** the delicate glass — the same thing liked in the first image.

**Drop:** the strength of the colour. Said about both images now, which makes it
a standing preference rather than a reaction to one picture: the *structure* of
this palette, not its saturation.

## Why this one matters more than the first

The first image was controls on a flat grey ground. This is glass **over a
photograph**, which is the case that actually decides whether any of it can be
used here — the app has a drifting sky behind the home screen and photographs in
the journal.

It works in this image. But look at why: the photo behind it is **blurred and
low-contrast**, a soft warm blob with no edges and no text. That is a
cooperative ground. It refines the earlier warning rather than cancelling it —
translucency survives a *quiet* background, not any background.

## The structure underneath the colour

Almost the whole panel is near-white. Colour appears in five places, and every
one of them is a thing with a single job:

- the primary action (violet)
- the secondary action (teal)
- the `+` that adds something (violet)
- the checkbox that is checked (violet)
- the progress under the selected tab (violet)

Nothing is coloured for decoration except the gradient card at the bottom, which
is also the least reusable thing in the picture.

**One saturated element per region, everything else near-white.** That is the
part to take. Turning the chroma down, as asked, does not break it — it is a
rule about *where* colour goes, and it survives the colours getting quieter.

## Glass containing glass

The outer panel is itself translucent, and the controls inside it are
translucent again. It holds because each layer is barely tinted; two washes at
this strength still read as one clean surface.

Worth noting for this app, where nesting goes deep — planning has a month panel
holding a week panel holding day chips. Three layers of glass at this app's
saturation would not stay legible. Another argument for the quieter colour.

## For the Hebrew

Text never sits on a saturated wash here. Labels are dark on near-white; the
only white-on-colour text is inside the two filled buttons, which are solid
enough to carry it.

That distinction has to hold in Hebrew too, where the letterforms are lighter
than the Latin ones in this image and lose to a busy ground sooner.
