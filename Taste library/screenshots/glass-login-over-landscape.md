# glass-login-over-landscape.jpg

A frosted card floating over a photograph of green hills at dusk: title,
subtitle, two fields, a checkbox, one green gradient button.

## What Asaf said

> אני אוהב את הרעיון הזה לכניסה לאפליקציה.

Sent together with a photograph of his own (`own photos/alpine-meadow-morning.jpg`),
which is what the reference is really about — not this card, but *his* card over
*that* picture.

## The one thing that has to be said first

**This app has no accounts.** Verified, not assumed: there is no sign-in, no
user record, no server holding anybody's data. The only two `type="password"`
fields in 1.1 MB of app protect a *backup file*, not an identity.

So "כניסה לאפליקציה" is almost certainly about **the arrival** — the first
moment of opening — rather than authentication. Which is the better idea anyway:
a login screen that logs nobody in is a lie told at the door.

The other reading is not fantasy either. If the app ever syncs across devices,
or becomes an App Store product with data that outlives one phone, accounts
arrive with it — and then this screen is real. Worth keeping both readings; only
the second one needs a password field.

## What makes the composition work

**The card is not centred.** It sits high, and the hills sweep out from under
it. The photograph gets to be a photograph rather than wallpaper behind a box.

**The blur is the whole trick.** Not transparency — *frost*. The landscape's
colours come through as soft bands, but no edge does, so nothing behind the card
competes with the text on it. This is the answer to the risk written down under
[[liquid-glass-panel]]: glass over a photograph works when the glass is blurred
enough to erase the photograph's detail.

**One thing is coloured and it is the way in.** Everything else is white on
frost; the button is a saturated green gradient. That is the same rule as
[[liquid-glass-panel]] — one saturated element per region — applied to a screen
with a single purpose.

**The green comes from the picture.** The button is not a brand colour dropped
on top; it is the meadow, concentrated. That is why it looks like it belongs.
And it is a technique that would carry straight to this app's own photograph.

## What would break in Hebrew

The fields put icons on the right — the person glyph, the eye. In RTL that is
where the *text* starts, so the icons want to move to the left and the
placeholders to align right. Small, but it is the kind of thing that looks
broken rather than untranslated if missed.
