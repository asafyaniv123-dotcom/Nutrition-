## What to notice

**THE LIGHT IS THE ONLY DIFFERENCE.** Nothing else changes between a done row
and an undone one - same surface, same type, same weight, same grey subtitle.
No tick, no filled checkbox, no coloured border, no strikethrough. State is
carried entirely by illumination.

**AND IT IS A LIGHT, NOT A COLOUR.** That is the whole craft of it. The glow
has a source - the control - and it behaves like one: brightest at the icon,
a halo immediately around it, then a wash that warms the surface near it and
dies out before the far end of the row. A row with an amber BACKGROUND would
be a different thing entirely and would look like a filled cell.

**THERE ARE DEGREES.** The bottom-middle row is faintly lit; the bottom-right
is fully lit, its icon amber and its glow reaching the row's edge. So the same
device says "a little" and "completely" without any second vocabulary.

**THE GROUND IS NEUTRAL AND PALE**, which is what lets one warm light read as
light at all. On a coloured ground it would read as a stain.

## Where it applies

**The questions in סיום יום** - his own suggestion, and it fits what the
reflection is. Measured: the reflection walks its questions one at a time from
RF_STAGE1 and then a bank of twenty-two more in RF_STAGE2, so there is no list
today on which a lit state could sit. The place it would live is a list of the
bank - the thing that shows what you have given something to and what is still
waiting - and that screen does not exist yet in that form.

Which makes this a reference for a screen to BUILD rather than a skin for one
that exists. Worth saying plainly, because the two are very different amounts
of work.

**And it is the right emotional shape for this app.** A tick says "task
complete". A light says "this one has something in it now". The daily
reflection is not a checklist and has never wanted to look like one; the page
warming up as it fills is a far better description of what is happening.

## The hard part

**Our ground is not neutral.** The home screen sits on a lavender-to-peach
gradient, and an amber glow on a warm mauve ground will muddy rather than
glow. Either the reflection gets a quieter ground of its own, or the light
takes a colour that separates from the page.

**Light alone cannot be the only signal.** Someone who cannot make out a soft
warm wash gets no state at all - and "answered" is not decoration. A second,
quieter cue has to ride along with it: the subtitle carrying the answer
itself, or the order of the list.
