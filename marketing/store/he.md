## Notes

- **No gendered address anywhere.** Hebrew marks gender on "you", on its
  verbs and on שלך, so the copy speaks in infinitives (לענות, לדלג, לצלם),
  impersonal forms (אפשר, מתחשק, נרשם, נלבש) and nouns. The promotional text
  uses the reader's own first person (מה אכלתי, איך זזתי, את מי פגשתי), which
  is neutral in the past tense and reads as the questions themselves.
  "Stays on your phone" became "stays on the phone"; "your own language"
  became "Hebrew or any other language".
- **"People: when you last spoke"** is מתי דיברנו לאחרונה, first-person
  plural: "we" is the reader and the other person, not the app addressing a
  crowd, and it is gender-free.
- **Name descriptor:** רפלקציה יומית is the term Hebrew wellness readers use
  for exactly this practice; יומן is the word people search, so it sits in
  the subtitle where it also carries the promise.
- **Subtitle:** "a journal that knows your day" → "a journal that knows the
  day that passed" — שלך would gender the reader, and "the day that passed"
  keeps the evening framing.
- **Beem** is written בים, as asked, and is masculine ("a little friend in
  the shape of a sun") to match how he is spoken of in the project. "Celebrates
  the days you show up" became "glad of every day of keeping at it" (התמדה),
  which is what the streak counts, without a verb aimed at the reader.
- **Headings** are short sentence-case Hebrew phrases; "YOURS" became פרטיות
  (privacy) because every Hebrew rendering of "yours" is gendered. The body
  promises only the journal, as in English.
- **Language list** opens with עברית for this reader and keeps the native
  names otherwise.
- **Keywords** avoid יומן, רפלקציה and יומית (already in name/subtitle) and
  favour the words Hebrew searchers type: מצב רוח, הכרת תודה, הרגלים, כושר,
  תזונה.
