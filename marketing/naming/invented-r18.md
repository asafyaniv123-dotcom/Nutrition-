# Name screen, round 18: ten invented words

Screened 24 Oct 2026. This is not legal advice and not a clearance search.

Candidates: Olimei, Kayoli, Nahari, Inaora, Tawanola, Merayu, Omnidia,
Ambovita, Unilumo, Todaluz. All ten had already passed the store and domain
screen: no App Store hit, no Play hit, and `.app` unused. The store title in
every case is "<Name>: Daily Reflection". The brief: one invented word, like
Duolingo, blended from roots in several languages, with the meaning a little
hidden.

## What was searched, and what could not be

- **US trademarks.** Each name got a quoted Trademarkia search,
  `https://www.trademarkia.com/search/trademarks?q=%22<name>%22`, and so did
  the spellings one letter off.
  - **All ten exact spellings returned 0 results.**
  - **Calibration:** the same quoted search returned real marks for near
    spellings (KAYALI, UNILUME, OLIMEL, MERAYA, NAHRI, INORA, OMNIVIA,
    OMNIDIAN, TODOLUZ). So the zeros are real zeros for the exact spelling.
    Trademarkia's "may be available" banner is boilerplate and was ignored.
  - **Near spellings searched:**
    - olimel, olimey
    - kayali, kayola
    - nahar, nahri
    - inaura, inora
    - tawanoa
    - meraya, merayo
    - omnidea, omnida, omnivia, omnidian, "omnia dia"
    - amovita, ambivita, ambovit
    - unilume, unilum
    - todalux, todoluz
- **Not reachable from this session:**
  - EUIPO TMview API (`https://www.tmdn.org/tmview/api/search/results?basicSearch=omnidia`):
    **connection reset**.
  - EUIPO eSearch (`https://euipo.europa.eu/eSearch/#basic/.../omnidia`):
    **connection reset**.
  - WIPO Global Brand Database
    (`https://branddb.wipo.int/en/similarname/results?strategy=exact&brandName=nahari`):
    an ALTCHA bot check that serves no results.
  - **So the EU, WIPO/Madrid, the UK, Japan (J-PlatPat), China (CNIPA) and
    Israel are NOT covered.** Run the finalist through TMview, the WIPO Global
    Brand Database and J-PlatPat by hand before filing.
- **Existing use.** Each name got a quoted web search, plus follow-ups on what
  came up. The search engine is US-only and indexes thinly. Nine of the ten
  returned no page about the exact word. Nahari is the exception, and it is a
  real name (see its section).
- **Dictionary check.** Merayu was checked against KBBI, the official
  Indonesian dictionary, through two mirrors:
  `https://kbbi.web.id/rayu` and `https://alkitab.sabda.org/lexicon.php?word=rayu`.
  The official site `kbbi.kemdikbud.go.id` did not resolve.
- **Pronunciation and meaning** come from knowledge of the languages, checked
  against the web where a claim was specific. **A native speaker of each
  language should confirm them before a decision.** This applies most to
  Japanese, Arabic and Chinese.

Classes that matter for this app:

| Class | Covers |
|---|---|
| 9 | software, apps |
| 42 | SaaS |
| 41 | education, wellness content |
| 44 | health, nutrition |
| 45 | personal and social services |

Verdicts:

- **CLEAR:** nothing relevant found.
- **CAUTION:** a similar mark, an unhelpful reading, or a name already in use.
  The section explains which.
- **CONFLICT:** one of three things:
  - the identical mark is live;
  - a near mark sits in class 9, 41, 42, 44 or 45 and the names would be
    confused;
  - the word itself is disqualifying.

Ratings run from 1 to 5:

- **Tongue:** 1 is awkward and 5 rolls off the tongue. Stress is shown in
  capitals.
- **Duolingo test:** 1 means no "aha" once explained, and 5 means it lands as
  well as duo + lingo.

## 1. Olimei

**Trademarks.**
- OLIMEI: 0 marks.
- OLIMEY: 0 marks.
- OLIMEL: 6 marks, all Baxter International, class 5 (an IV nutrition
  product). One is live/pending and five are dead. Class 5 is not this app's
  class, but Baxter's product is clinical nutrition, which sits near our food
  logging. It is a different word with a different ending: low risk.

**Existing use.**
- No page about the exact word.
- The nearest results were Olimega (a Quebec camelina business) and Oliomio
  (Chilean olive equipment).
- It was not found as a surname or a given name. The Tamil roots are real:
  *oli* (ஒளி) means light, and *mei* (மெய்) means body and also truth.

**Sound and meaning.**
- **EN:** oh-lee-MAY. A one-time hearer writes "Olimay" or "Olimey" about half
  the time.
- **HE:** אולימיי. It brushes *olami* (עולמי, "global") and *olim* (עולים,
  "immigrants"). Neutral.
- **AR:** أوليماي. Nothing bad.
- **DE:** "Olimai", which is fine. *Mai* is May.
- **ES, PT:** oh-lee-MAY. Clean.
- **IT:** "oli miei" (my oils) sits faintly underneath. Harmless.
- **FR:** "Olimeï". Fine.
- **JA:** オリメイ. *Mei* (明) reads as "bright", a welcome accident. *Orime*
  (折り目, a crease) is a faint echo.
- **ZH:** 奥利美 (ào lì měi). *Měi* (美) means beautiful. Good.
- **Spellable once heard?** Moderately. The ending (-ei, -ay, -ey) is the
  weak point.

**Tongue: 4/5.** o-li-MEI: three light open syllables with no consonant
clusters.

**Duolingo test: 3/5.**
- "Light + body (and truth)" is a lovely gloss for a journal that connects
  body and mind.
- But the roots are Tamil, which almost none of the eleven audiences know, so
  the "aha" has to be told, not recognised.
- In Japanese and Chinese, *mei* reads as "bright" and "beautiful" on its own,
  which helps.

**Verdict: CLEAR.** The only issue is the Baxter OLIMEL echo in class 5,
which is distant.

## 2. Kayoli

**Trademarks.**
- KAYOLI: 0 marks.
- KAYOLA: 1 dead mark, class 29.
- **KAYALI: 2 live registrations, class 3**, held by Kayali Brands Holdings.
  This is Mona Kattan's perfume house, spun out of Huda Beauty. It is a
  well-known consumer brand. Its class (cosmetics) is not ours, but the names
  differ by one vowel.

**Existing use.**
- No page about the exact word.
- Searches drift to **Kayali** (the perfume; Arabic *khayali*, "my
  imagination") and to **Kayole**, a low-income neighbourhood of Nairobi.

**Sound and meaning.**
- **EN:** kai-YO-lee. A one-time hearer may write "Kayolee", "Kaioli" or
  "Kayoley". The spelling is fair to weak.
- **AR:** كايولي. It sits next to *Kayali*, which Arabic-speaking women in
  particular know as a perfume, and to *khayali*. Not bad, just borrowed.
- **HE:** קאיולי. Nothing bad.
- **DE, FR, IT, ES, PT:** clean.
  - Spanish *cayo* means a key or islet.
  - Portuguese *caiu* means "fell". It is a faint echo of "Kayo-".
- **JA:** カヨリ. It reads like a girl's name (*Kaori*, *Kayo*), plus
  *kayoi* (通い, "commuting"). Friendly.
- **ZH:** 卡优丽. Fine.

**Tongue: 4/5.** ka-YO-li flows. The y-glide is easy everywhere.

**Duolingo test: 2/5.**
- Sanskrit *kāya* (body) + Tamil *oli* (light): both roots are obscure.
- Nobody will recognise "body" in *kay-*, so the "aha" is a footnote rather
  than a delight.

**Verdict: CAUTION.** The trademark path is clear. But it sits one vowel from
a famous Gulf-born perfume brand, and that hurts recall and searchability,
especially in Arabic.

## 3. Nahari

**Trademarks.**
- NAHARI: 0 marks.
- NAHAR: 0 marks.
- **NAHRI: 1 live registration, classes 9, 35 and 41**, held by HCPro LLC
  (healthcare compliance and training). The serial number is 98625030,
  registered 18 Mar 2025. That is one letter off, in two of our classes.

**Existing use.** This is a real, established word and name in three of our
markets.
- **Israel.** Nahari (נהרי) is a Yemenite-Jewish surname. **Meshulam Nahari**
  is a Shas politician: a former minister and Knesset member, whose
  controversies include criticising Gilad Shalit for going to the beach
  instead of the synagogue.
  - Sources: `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meshulam_Nahari` and
    `https://www.israelnationalnews.com/tags/Meshulam_Nahari`.
  - Hebrew speakers, who are the app's home audience, will hear a politician's
    surname.
- **Japan.** **Nahari-chō (奈半利町)** is a town in Kōchi Prefecture with
  about 2,700 people, on the Shikoku pilgrimage route.
  - Source: `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahari,_K%C5%8Dchi`.
  - A Japanese reader sees a place name, not *nagi* + *hari*.
- **Arabic.** نهاري (*nahārī*) means "of the daytime, diurnal". Nahar/Nahari is
  also a Bangladeshi and Arab name.
  - Source: `https://en.geneanet.org/surnames/NAHAR`.
  - The meaning is pleasant ("of the day"), but it is a dictionary adjective,
    not an invented word.

**Sound and meaning.**
- **EN:** na-HA-ree. Easy to spell from hearing.
- **HE:** "neharí". Surname, river (*nahar*), and the light root (*nehora*).
  The light reading is nice; the Shas surname dominates.
- **AR:** "daytime". Fine but generic.
- **JA:** ナハリ. A town name.
- **ES, PT, FR, IT:** the H is silent in Romance languages, so they say
  "Naari". Spanish says it with the H as a soft j. Fine.
- **DE:** clean.
- **ZH:** 那哈里. Fine.

**Tongue: 5/5.** na-HA-ri is effortless.

**Duolingo test: 2/5.**
- *Nagi* + *hari* is not visible in the word: the *g* is gone.
- The meaning a listener actually gets is "daytime" (Arabic) or "a surname"
  (Hebrew). That is a dictionary word, not a hidden fusion.

**Verdict: CONFLICT.** It is not invented: it is an Israeli politician's
surname, a Japanese town and an Arabic adjective. On top of that, NAHRI is
live in classes 9 and 41.

## 6. Merayu

**Trademarks.**
- MERAYU: 0 marks.
- MERAYO: 0 marks.
- MERAYA: 2 marks, classes 5 and 20. Not ours.

**Existing use: it is a real Malay/Indonesian word, verified.** The KBBI
entries (mirror `https://alkitab.sabda.org/lexicon.php?word=rayu`) give:

- From *rayu*¹:
  - *merayu*: "merasa pilu; terharu" (to feel sad, to be moved).
- From *rayu*²:
  - *merayu*: "menyenangkan hati (menyedapkan hati, menawan)" (to charm,
    please);
  - *merayu*: "**membujuk (memikat) dng kata-kata manis**" (to coax or lure
    with sweet words). The dictionary's own example is *ia merayu gadis itu
    dng kata-kata manis*: "he sweet-talked the girl".
  - *merayu*: "mengajukan permohonan" (to plead, appeal). In Malay this is
    also the legal word for filing an appeal.

So for roughly 280 million Malay and Indonesian speakers, "Merayu" is an
everyday verb meaning **to sweet-talk or seduce, or to plead**. *Rayuan
gombal* is "cheap flattery". Those languages are not among the eleven, but
Indonesia is a top-five app market, and the store listing is global.

**Sound and meaning.**
- **EN:** meh-RAI-yoo. Easy to spell.
- **ES:** heard as "**me rayo**". In Spain, *rayarse* is slang for getting
  worked up or overthinking ("me rayo" = "I'm spiralling"). For a reflection
  journal that is half an in-joke and half a warning. Comic.
- **PT, IT, FR, DE:** clean.
  - Spanish/Portuguese *mera* means "mere".
  - Italian *mera* means "mere, pure".
- **AR:** ميرايو. Levantine and Egyptian *mrāya*/*mirāya* (مراية, "mirror")
  sits close. That is a nice accident for a reflection app.
- **HE:** מראיו. Nothing bad.
- **JA:** メラユ. *Mera-mera* (めらめら) is "blazing". Fine.
- **ZH:** 美拉玉. Fine.

**Tongue: 4/5.** me-RA-yu flows easily.

**Duolingo test: 3/5.**
- Greek *mera* (day) + Sanskrit *āyu* (life): "the day is life" lands quite
  well.
- Greek speakers get *mera* immediately.

**Verdict: CONFLICT.** The trademark path is clear, but the name is an
existing everyday word meaning "to seduce/sweet-talk" in Indonesian and Malay.
For a personal journal holding intimate data, that cannot be explained away in
a large market. The Spanish "me rayo" adds a comic layer.

## 7. Omnidia

**Trademarks.**
- OMNIDIA: 0 marks.
- OMNIDA, OMNIDEA, "OMNIA DIA": 0 marks.
- OMNIDIAN: live, class 35 (solar asset services).
- OMNIVIA: live, class 5; and a dead class 9 mark held by Infineon.
- The bare **OMNI-** prefix is among the most crowded in classes 9 and 42.
  Hundreds of OMNI marks exist, which makes it weak to enforce and easy to
  attack. This was not counted here; the crowding is general knowledge.

**Existing use.**
- No page about the exact word.
- Search drifts to:
  - Omnidian (solar);
  - Omnidata (a 1980s word-processor maker);
  - Omdia (Informa's tech research arm);
  - OMNIA Protocol (crypto).
- The neighbourhood is tech and enterprise, not personal.

**Sound and meaning.**
- **EN:** om-NID-ee-a.
  - It reads as "omni-media" at a glance.
  - Spoken, its ending rhymes with **Nvidia** (en-VID-ee-a), so "Omnidia"
    sounds like an Nvidia product or a GPU line. That is a real risk.
  - "Omnidian" and "Omdia" add to the B2B feel.
- **ES, PT:** "omni-DÍ-a" means "every day" and is transparent. They get it
  instantly.
- **IT:** "omni" plus *dì* (day). Close.
- **FR:** "omnidia". *Omni-* is a known prefix. Neutral.
- **DE:** neutral and technical.
- **HE:** אומנידיה. Reads as tech.
- **AR:** أومنيديا. Fine, though *-dia* resembles *media* (ميديا).
- **JA:** オムニディア. Reads as a tech or media company.
- **ZH:** 欧姆尼迪亚. Long. *Ōumǔ* (欧姆) is "ohm", the electrical unit.
- **Spellable once heard?** Yes, mostly. "Omnidea" is the slip.

**Tongue: 4/5.** om-NI-di-a is smooth, Latin-shaped and familiar.

**Duolingo test: 4/5.**
- This is the most Duolingo-like structure in the list: two well-known roots,
  *omni* (all) and *día* (day), making "every day".
- For Spanish and Portuguese speakers the meaning is not hidden at all.
- For English speakers it is a genuine small aha.
- But the aha is "every day", which describes a calendar, not reflection or
  light.

**Verdict: CAUTION.**
- There is no identical mark.
- But it sounds like enterprise tech: Nvidia, Omdia, Omnidian, omni-media.
- It sits in the most crowded prefix in software, so it will be hard to own.
- It feels like a B2B data platform, not a warm evening journal.

## 10. Todaluz

**Trademarks.**
- TODALUZ, TODALUX: 0 marks.
- **TODOLUZ: 2 live US registrations, class 11** (lamps), held by Foshan Nanhai
  Baolusi Dengshi Chang.

**Existing use.**
- **Todoluz is a Spanish lighting-store chain**: four stores in Granada,
  Málaga, Madrid and Jaén, founded in 1995, with an online shop.
  - Source: `https://www.tiendeo.com/ajo/todoluz`.
- So the one-letter-off name is a lighting brand in Spain and a lamp mark in
  the US.

**Sound and meaning.**
- **ES:** "toda luz", heard as a grammatical-ish phrase ("all light"). It is
  not hidden and not invented, it is next to Todoluz, and Spanish speakers may
  "correct" it to "Todoluz".
- **PT (BR):** "toda luz" is an ordinary phrase ("every light"). Impossible to
  own as a word.
- **HE:** "**toda**" (תודה) is "thank you", so it reads as "thanks, Luz" (Luz
  is a woman's name). That is a charming gratitude echo for Hebrew speakers.
- **EN:** TOH-da-looz. Said fast it is "today lose" or "toad-a-loose", which
  is a little clumsy.
- **DE, FR:** neutral, with "luz" exotic.
- **IT:** "tutta luce" is understood. Fine.
- **AR:** تودالوز. Fine. *Lawz* (لوز) means almonds. Harmless.
- **JA:** トダルス or トダルズ. Toda (戸田) is a common surname and a city.
- **ZH:** 托达卢斯. Fine.
- **Spellable once heard?** In English it is spelled "Todaluse" or
  "Todalooz". In Spanish and Portuguese it is spelled correctly, but as two
  words.

**Tongue: 3/5.** to-da-LUZ. The final "z" (said "s" in Latin America, "th" in
Spain) is unstable across languages.

**Duolingo test: 2/5.** For Iberian languages there is nothing hidden: it is a
plain phrase. For everyone else, "toda" is opaque. The Hebrew "thank you"
reading is a lovely accident, but it is not the intended meaning.

**Verdict: CAUTION, leaning CONFLICT.** It is a plain phrase in two of the
eleven languages, and one letter from a Spanish lighting chain and a live US
lamp mark.
