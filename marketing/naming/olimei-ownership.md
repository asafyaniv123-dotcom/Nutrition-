# Owning the name Olimei

Research date: 24 Oct 2026. This is research, not legal advice. The fee figures
are the ones published at the sources cited, and several of those sources do not
agree with each other. Before paying anything, check each number against the
office's own fee page on the day you file.

---

## 0. Summary

- **No large company uses Olimei, and none uses anything identical to it.** The
  closest big mark is **OLIMEL**, Baxter's intravenous (IV) nutrition line. It
  differs by one letter and sits in the medical part of "nutrition". That is the
  one name worth planning around. Everything else is either small or clearly in a
  different field.
- **Olimei is a coined word**, which puts it at the strong end of the
  distinctiveness scale. On distinctiveness it should register easily. The only
  real question is the relative one: how close it is to OLIMEL.
- **The cheapest way to own it:** file an Israeli application in classes 9 and 42
  now (about NIS 2,800–3,100 in fees). Then, within 6 months, file a Madrid
  international application based on it, designating the US, EU, UK, Japan and
  China while claiming the Israeli date (about CHF 3,700 in WIPO fees).
- **olimei.com is held by a domain reseller and is for sale at $3,295.**
  olimei.app and olimei.io are free. olimei.net is registered by someone else.

## 2. Registrability

**The verdict: a coined (fanciful) word, so inherently distinctive and strong.**
"Olimei" has no dictionary meaning in English, and it does not describe nutrition,
fitness or software. Under every trademark system that places it at the top of
the distinctiveness scale (fanciful, then arbitrary, suggestive, descriptive,
generic). Absolute grounds, meaning descriptiveness, should not be a problem
anywhere.

The only realistic objections are **relative**: an earlier similar mark for
similar goods.

- **OLIMEL in class 5.** An examiner in the EU or US is unlikely to cite it
  against classes 9 and 42, but an opposition from Baxter is possible. Keep the
  goods away from medicine.
- **AOLIMEI in class 5 (US only).** Same reasoning.
- Watch the **meaning in Japanese and Chinese.** "mei" reads as 美 (beautiful)
  and "Oli-" as a prefix. That does not make the word descriptive, but in China
  a squatter could file a Chinese-character version. Consider later also
  registering the katakana form (オリメイ) and a chosen Chinese-character form.

**Nice classes to file (12th edition):**

| Class | Covers | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **9** | Downloadable mobile application software for nutrition tracking, fitness training, journaling and daily reflection; downloadable software for health and wellness tracking | **Essential**: this is the app itself |
| **42** | Software as a service (SaaS); providing temporary use of non-downloadable software for … (same fields); hosting of online platforms | **Essential**: covers the web or PWA version, the server side and any future web product |
| 41 | Providing fitness training and coaching information online; providing non-downloadable online publications; education in nutrition and fitness | **Optional**: worth adding if the app's coaching or content is a selling point. In the US each extra class costs $350; in the EU the third class costs €150. |
| 44 | Providing information about nutrition, dietary advice, health and wellness information | **Optional, and the class closest to Baxter.** If you add it, word it as wellness and lifestyle information, not medical or clinical nutrition. |
| 5 | Supplements, dietetic substances | **Do not file.** Only if you ever sell supplements; it lands straight on OLIMEL and AOLIMEI. |

Recommendation: **classes 9 and 42 now.** Add 41 or 44 in a later filing once
the product shape is settled. A later filing is a new application with a new
date, so add them early if you are sure you will need them.

## 3. How to own it, step by step (an Israeli individual)

### 3a. The Israeli national application (ILPO)

- **Online:** yes. ILPO takes trademark applications through its online system,
  **trademarksonline.justice.gov.il**. WIPO's 2024 report from ILPO says all new
  applications go through that platform. An individual may file without an
  attorney; attorney representation is mandatory only for corporations and for
  attorneys filing on behalf of others, per a 2020 guide.
- **Official fee:** the published figures disagree. They range from **NIS
  1,604–1,795 for the first class and NIS 1,205–1,350 for each additional
  class.** Two classes therefore come to **about NIS 2,800–3,150**. An optional
  ILPO pre-filing similarity search costs about NIS 650 per class (2020 figure);
  expedited examination about NIS 750 per class.
- **Timeline:** examination usually takes many months. A 3-month opposition
  period follows acceptance and publication. Expect roughly **a year or more** to
  registration, faster with expedited examination. *(This is an estimate; ILPO
  does not publish a guaranteed time.)*
- **Why file in Israel first, even though Israel is not the main market:**
  1. It gives a **Paris Convention priority date.** For 6 months, a filing in any
     member country (US, EU, Japan, China …) can claim the Israeli filing date.
  2. It is the **basic mark** that a Madrid international application must rest
     on. WIPO says Israeli Madrid e-filing (live since 19 Jul 2023) takes either
     an Israeli application or an Israeli registration as the basic mark.

### 3b. Madrid Protocol through ILPO: one application, many countries

Filed online with WIPO's Madrid e-Filing, with ILPO as office of origin. Fees
are paid to WIPO in Swiss francs (CHF):

**The catches with Madrid:**

- **Dependency, or "central attack".** For 5 years the international
  registration depends on the Israeli basic mark. If the Israeli application is
  refused or cancelled, every designation falls with it. Each can then be
  converted into a national filing that keeps its date, but at national cost.
  With a coined word and clean classes the risk is small.
- **US: a US-licensed attorney becomes mandatory** for foreign-domiciled
  applicants as soon as the USPTO issues any office action, which is common.
  Budget **$500–1,500** for US counsel.
- **Other offices' refusals** (China and Japan especially) are answered by a
  local agent, at **a few hundred dollars each** if they occur.
- **EU:** one designation covers all 27 member states. It does not cover the UK,
  Switzerland or Norway.

### 3c. Or file directly in each office

| Office | Official fee, 2 classes | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| USPTO | $700 ($350 per class; +$200 per class if you write your own goods description) | **A US attorney is required from day one** for a foreign-domiciled applicant, so add $800–2,500 in attorney fees |
| EUIPO | €900 (€850 + €50) | Online. An individual from outside the EU must appoint an EU representative for anything beyond filing. EUIPO's SME Fund has in past years reimbursed part of these fees; it applies to EU SMEs, so check eligibility. |
| UKIPO | £220 (£170 + £50 online). Reported as £205 + £60 from 1 Apr 2026. | A UK address for service is needed |

Filing directly costs more in agent fees, but it avoids the 5-year dependency.
For one person, Madrid is usually simpler and cheaper.

### 3e. Do you need a lawyer, and what does a clearance search cost?

- **Not legally required** for the Israeli or Madrid filing by an individual.
  **Required** for the US the moment anything goes wrong, or from the start if
  filing directly.
- **A professional clearance search is worth having** because the plan rests on
  one judgement call: OLIMEL. Prices quoted by vendors and firms: **a basic
  search $100–500; a comprehensive one** (registers, common law, domains, app
  stores) **$300–1,500** for one territory. A multi-country screening by an
  Israeli IP firm usually costs about $1,000–2,500. The ILPO's own similarity
  search is about NIS 650 per class.

## 4. Protection before registration

- **Israel:** registration is the main route to enforceable rights. An
  unregistered user can sue only in passing off, which requires reputation and
  is hard for a new app. **The filing date is what counts**, which is why filing
  now matters more than launching first.
- **US:** common-law rights arise from **actual use in commerce**, but only in
  the area and market where the mark is actually known. For an app, that is
  weak and hard to prove. An "intent to use" US application, or a Madrid
  designation, reserves the date even before launch.
- **EU, China and Japan:** first to file. Using a name gives almost no right
  against a later registrant. **China in particular is where squatters register
  foreign app names**, and "Olimei" is already used there on clothing. This is
  why China belongs in the first filing.
- **™:** anyone can put ™ after a name they claim as a mark, at no cost and with
  no filing. It puts others on notice and helps show the date of first use. It
  creates no rights of its own. **®** may be used only after registration, and
  only in the countries where the mark is registered. Using ® falsely is an
  offence in some countries.
- **App Store and Google Play names:**
  - **Apple:** guideline 2.3.7 requires a unique app name, and the name is
    reserved for the localisations saved when the app record is created in App
    Store Connect. That is **a contractual reservation, not property.** Apple's
    **App Name Dispute** form lets a *trademark holder* complain that an app is
    blocking their mark, and Apple then asks the blocking developer to resolve
    it. Without a registration, you are the one who can lose the name. With one,
    you can take it back from a copycat.
  - **Google Play:** titles do not have to be unique; only the package name
    (`com.…`) does. Copycats can use the same title, and Google acts on
    trademark complaints, though not consistently (in *Spy Phone Labs v. Google*
    it declined to judge the merits).
  - **The conclusion:** store uniqueness protects nothing on its own. The
    registration is what makes both stores' complaint processes work for you.

## 7. Action plan

### This week (about $50–100, plus optionally $3,295)

1. **Run the free searches by hand** (1 hour, $0): the WIPO Global Brand
   Database, TMview, USPTO, CNIPA and J-PlatPat, for OLIMEI and its variants in
   classes 9, 42, 41 and 44. If an identical mark turns up in class 9 or 42
   anywhere, stop and reconsider before spending.
2. **Register olimei.app** (~$15–20) and **olimei.io** (~$50) if you want it.
   Try to check and register **olimei.co** (~$30) and **olimei.co.il** (~NIS 80).
3. **Decide on olimei.com:** $3,295 from HugeDomains, or the 24-month plan. It
   is not urgent unless someone else buys it first; it has been parked since
   2012.
4. **Claim the handles** ($0): Instagram, TikTok, X, YouTube, Facebook, Threads,
   LinkedIn, Reddit, GitHub, Discord.
5. **Create the App Store Connect app record "Olimei"**, which reserves the name
   on the App Store, and pick the Google Play package name ($0 extra on the
   existing accounts).
6. Start writing **Olimei™** on the website and in store copy ($0).

### This month (about NIS 3,000 alone, or about NIS 6,000–10,000 with an attorney and a search)

7. **Optional, but recommended because of OLIMEL:** a **professional clearance
   opinion** from an Israeli trademark attorney, covering IL, US, EU, UK, CN
   and JP: about **$1,000–2,500**. Ask them specifically about OLIMEL (Baxter)
   and the class 44 wording.
8. **File the Israeli application** online at trademarksonline.justice.gov.il:
   word mark OLIMEI (standard characters, which protects every font and logo),
   **classes 9 and 42**: about **NIS 2,800–3,150**. Do it yourself, or through
   the attorney from step 7 for about NIS 2,000–4,000 more. **This date is now
   your priority date in every Paris country for 6 months.**

### Within 6 months of the Israeli filing, and before launch (about $4,500–5,500, plus counsel when needed)

9. **File the Madrid international application** through ILPO's Madrid
   e-Filing, based on the Israeli application and **claiming its priority**:
   US, EU, UK, Japan and China, 2 classes, **about CHF 3,600–3,700 (≈ $4,500)**.
   Add Korea (CHF 300), Canada (368) and Australia (434) if those markets
   matter at launch.
10. **Line up a US attorney** for the first USPTO office action (**$500–1,500**),
    and expect local-agent fees of a few hundred dollars for each refusal in
    China or Japan.
11. If the brand will show Chinese or Japanese script, **decide on the 美-style
    Chinese form and the katakana form** and file them in China and Japan as
    separate marks (about CHF 330 and 430 more). In China this is the main
    defence against squatters.

### After launch and ongoing

12. **Keep using the name exactly as registered**, and keep dated evidence
    (screenshots, store pages, download counts). The US requires a declaration
    of use between years 5 and 6, and a mark left unused for 5 years becomes
    vulnerable to cancellation in most countries.
13. **Set up a watch.** Free: a Google Alert for "Olimei", and a monthly check
    of the App Store and Play for the name. Paid: a professional watch service
    at about $200–500 a year.
14. **Once registered:** switch to ® in the registered countries, and use the
    Apple App Name Dispute form and Google Play's trademark complaint form
    against copycats.
15. Renewals: Israel every 10 years; Madrid every 10 years at WIPO.

**Total realistic budget for owning Olimei in the six markets that matter: about
$6,000–10,000 over 18 months**, of which about $5,300 is official fees.
Optionally add $3,295 for olimei.com.
