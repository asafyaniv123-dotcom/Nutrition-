### OLIMEL (Baxter): how close is it, really?

- **What it is:** a triple-chamber IV bag of amino acids, glucose and lipids (parenteral
  nutrition) for hospital patients who cannot eat. Authorised in France since 21 Jul 2008, sold
  in Europe, Asia-Pacific, Latin America and Canada.
  https://www.biospace.com/baxter-international-inc-introduces-olimel-in-canada-as-the-first-triple-chamber-bag-for-nutrition-375540 ,
  https://www.codifa.it/farmaci/o/olimel-emulsione-poliaminoacidi-e-glucosio-monoidrato-e-olio-di-oliva-e-olio-di-soia-per-uso-parenterale-e-elettroliti-soluzioni-nutrizionali-parenterali
- **US status:** five applications abandoned (2007, 2011, 2014, 2018, 2021, all for no statement
  of use). The sixth, **serial 99502836**, filed 18 Nov 2025 on intent-to-use, allowed, first SOU
  extension granted 22 Jul 2026. Goods: "Parenteral pharmaceuticals, namely, intravenous
  nutritional solutions sold in mixing containers having two or more chambers."
  https://www.trademarkia.com/olimel-99502836
- **Sight:** one letter apart, and the differing letters are I and L, which are nearly the same
  glyph in many sans-serifs. OLIMEI / OLIMEL are visually close; this is the real similarity.
- **Sound:** o-li-MAY vs o-li-MEL. Close but distinct; a final /l/ is clearly heard.
- **Meaning:** neither has one in English. No conceptual link.
- **Goods:** prescription IV drugs bought by hospital pharmacies vs a consumer journal app
  (class 9 / 42). Different buyers, channels and classes. The one bridge is that the app **logs
  food**, so "nutrition" is in both stories.
- **Verdict:** low risk for an app in class 9/42, **provided** Olimei never files in class 5 or
  44, and never markets itself as medical or clinical nutrition. Baxter's EU and national
  registrations of OLIMEL were **not** verified (see below) and almost certainly exist, since the
  product has been sold in Europe since 2008; a well-known-mark argument there is the residual risk.

## 4. Meaning

### The Tamil, verified

- **ஒளி (oḷi)** — Madras University *Tamil Lexicon* p. 609: "Light, brightness; splendour;
  brilliancy", also sun, moon, star. Wiktionary adds lamp, sunshine, fame.
  https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/tamil-lex_query.py?qs=ஒளி&searchhws=yes ,
  https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ஒளி
  - Same spelling, second etymology: **"to hide, conceal; a hiding place"**. Context makes
    "light" the dominant reading, but the homograph exists.
  - **The romanisation is the catch.** The light word has a retroflex **ḷ** (ள). Plain "oli" is
    the standard romanisation of a different word, **ஒலி (oli) = "sound, noise"** (வானொலி,
    vāṉoli, is "radio"). https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ஒலி . A Tamil reader seeing "Oli" in
    Latin letters will not know which is meant; written in Tamil script it must be ஒளி.
- **மெய் (mey)** — *Tamil Lexicon* p. 3336: 1. **truth, reality** (the main sense, Kural 354);
  2. soul; 3. consciousness; 4. **body** (marked euphemistic/formal, Kural 619); 5. breast;
  6. consonant (grammar). https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/tamil-lex_query.py?qs=மெய்&searchhws=yes ,
  https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/மெய் . Standard romanisation is "mey"; "mei" is acceptable.
  "Body" is real but literary; "truth" is what a Tamil speaker hears first.
- **As one word:** ஒளிமெய் is not a dictionary word (exact-phrase search: nothing). It reads as a
  transparent compound, "light-truth" / "luminous body". Nearby, and favourable: **ஒளிமயமான
  (oḷimayamāṉa) "radiant, full of light"**, the title of the 1977 Tamil film *Olimayamana
  Ethirkalam* ("A Radiant Future"). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olimayamana_Ethirkalam .
  To a Tamil ear "Olimay" sounds like "radiant", which is a happy accident for a sun logo.

**Comic echoes in English:** "Oli" reads as a nickname (fine). "Olive" and "Olympia" are near in
shape, not in sound. "Oh lame" would need the *i* dropped and an *l* added after the
vowel, so it does not arise naturally; no source mentions it. "Oh my" is
audible inside "o-li-MAY" and is friendly. Nothing vulgar was found in any of the eleven languages.

### How readers will say it from the spelling

The problem letters are **ei**:

| Reader | ei is | Likely | Stress |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | ay (vein), ee (receive), eye (height) — no rule | **o-li-MAY** or **O-li-mee**; some o-LIM-ee | unsure |
| German | always /aɪ/ "eye" | **o-li-MY** | last |
| French | /ɛ/ | **o-li-MÈ** | last (French always) |
| Spanish / Italian / Portuguese / Romanian | /ei/ | **o-li-MÉI** ✓ | last or penultimate |
| Dutch | /ɛi/ | close to o-li-MAY ✓ | |
| Japanese | メイ | o-ri-MEI ✓ (long *mē*) | flat |

So the spelling delivers the intended sound in the Romance languages, Dutch, Japanese and
Chinese; English is a coin toss; **German and French will consistently say something else.**

**Official pronunciation: o-li-MAY, /ˌoʊ.liˈmeɪ/, three syllables, stress on the last**, rhyming
with "today". It is what the Romance languages produce unaided, it keeps the Tamil *mey*, and
"…MAY" is easy to teach in one line ("Olimei, rhymes with *oh my, today*"). Put it on the store
page, in the first-run welcome, and say it out loud in the first video. Accept that German
speakers will say "Oli-Mai" and do not fight it.

## 5. Spelling: what people will type after hearing it once

olemay, ollimay, olimai. Searching in a store forgives some of this; a typed URL does not.

- Of these, olimay.com and olimey.com are **taken** (olimey.com since April 2026, "launching
  soon" — keep an eye on it).
- **Hold olimay.app** (free) and redirect it.
- Do **not** buy olymei.com: it is someone else's registered mark.
- Store listing: put "Olimay" and "Olimey" in the App Store keyword field (100 chars) and the
  Play description so a misspelt search still finds it.
- Handles: grab **@olimay** where it is free if cheap, but the brand handle should stay one
  spelling everywhere.

## Verdict: **GO WITH CARE**

Nothing found says no. The US register is clean for OLIMEI and every spelling of it, no app or
wellness product uses the name anywhere, the store search is empty, and the meaning is lovely and
checks out in the dictionary. The care is about what this screen could not reach, and about the
name's two soft spots.

### The three biggest risks

1. **The registers outside the US are unchecked, and OLIMEL sits one letter away.** EUIPO,
   TMview, WIPO, UK, Japan, China and Israel all blocked automated access. Baxter's OLIMEL
   (IV nutrition, class 5) is in Europe since 2008 and almost certainly registered there; I/L
   are near-identical glyphs. Low risk in class 9/42, but it must be cleared by an attorney's
   EU + TMview + CNIPA search before the store listing, and Olimei should never file in class 5
   or 44 or describe itself as clinical nutrition.
2. **The .com is not yours and the short handles are split.** olimei.com is parked at HugeDomains
   for $3,295; @olimei is a private person on Instagram and on TikTok; olimei.net is someone's
   live site. Without the .com, olimei.app becomes the primary address and every network wears
   the suffix "app".
3. **The spelling does not carry the sound.** German says o-li-MY, French o-li-MÈ, English
   splits; and in Tamil, the language the name comes from, "oli" in Latin letters is the
   standard spelling of *ஒலி* "sound", not *ஒளி* "light". The story has to be told (in Tamil
   script, with ள) rather than left to the letters.
