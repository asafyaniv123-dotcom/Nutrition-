# Greek and Latin roots for an invented name

The brief: an evening-reflection journal (food, body, movement, people, mood), a small sun for a logo, and a
name invented by blending two roots. This file lists **38 roots**, sorted best first. Each row gives the
**building-block** rating (1–5): how easily the root sits inside a name that has to be said in eleven
languages. 5 means all open syllables (consonant + vowel), no clusters, no sounds Japanese or Arabic lack
(*th*, *ps*, *mn*, final *x*). Within a rating, the roots with fewer collisions come first.

The brand checks were run on 24 Oct 2026 with WebSearch (US results). Where a row says *"not searched"*, the
note comes from general knowledge and should be checked before it is relied on. The foreign-meaning notes
are a first pass by one researcher, not a native-speaker review. The Hebrew, Arabic and Japanese ones in
particular should go past a native speaker before a name is chosen.

**Stress** is in CAPITALS. Where the ancient and the modern Greek pronunciations differ, both are given.

## What the table says

- **The four free, open-syllable roots that carry the brief** are *mera* (day), *sera* (evening), *anima*
  (soul/breath) and *luce* (light). *Galene* (calm) and *hemera* (day) are close behind. Each is short,
  pronounceable from Tokyo to Riyadh, and not held by a wellness app the search could find.
- **The obvious Greek roots are spent.** Kairos, Psyche, Soma, Helios, Eos and Mneme all already name
  journals, wellness apps or magazines. Kairos is a direct competitor.
- **Two roots conflict with nutrition products in particular:** *Lumen* (a metabolism tracker) and *Zoe* (a
  personalised-nutrition app). Avoid both even inside a blend.
- **Sun roots are the weak spot.** *Sol* is sweet but crowded, and *sola* means "alone". *Helios* is taken.
  *Ilios* sounds like *ileus*. Light (*luce*) and day (*mera*) carry the sun better than the word for sun
  does, and the logo already says "sun".

## Eight suggested two-root blends

All eight are made of open syllables except where noted, and none was trademark-searched. That check, and a
native-speaker reading in each of the eleven languages, comes before any shortlist.

| Blend | Roots | Say it | Why it works | Watch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lucera** | luce + sera | lu-CHÉ-ra / lu-SÉ-ra | "Evening light". It echoes ES *lucero*, the bright star (Venus) of dusk and dawn, which is a sun-adjacent image. | It is a town in Apulia. *Lucera* means "skylight" in some ES regions (harmless). |
| **Animera** | anima + (he)mera | a-ni-MÉ-ra | "The soul of the day". Body and mind plus the day. It has *era* inside it, which suggests time accumulating. | It is four syllables. In JA, check that it does not drift to アニメ. |
| **Kalisera** | kalos + sera | ka-li-SÉ-ra | It echoes Greek *kalispéra*, "good evening", without being the phrase. A warm evening greeting is exactly the app's moment. | *Kali-* is also the Hindu goddess Kali and potassium (*kalium*). Check the Greek ear: it may sound like a misspelling. |
| **Lumera** | lumen + (he)mera | lu-MÉ-ra | "Light of the day". Short, soft, all open syllables. | It sits close to Lumen, the nutrition device. Test whether *Lumera* reads as derivative. |
| **Animora** | anima + (h)ora / memoria | a-ni-MÓ-ra | "Soul + hour / memory". PT *mora* = "lives (in)", ES *mora* = blackberry, both gentle. | ES *mora* also means "delay in payment" (*en mora*), a financial term. |
| **Galora** | galene + (h)ora | ga-LÓ-ra | "The calm hour". English hears *galore* (abundance), which is positive. | It may read as a fantasy name. *Galore* doubles the l in some ears. |
| **Solena** | sol + (ga)lene / eirene | so-LÉ-na | "Sun + calm". It sounds like a gentle given name, and *sol* is still legible. | It is close to ES *solemne* and *sola* ("alone"). Several cosmetics brands use similar names (not searched). |
| **Seramí** / **Serami** | sera + (h)emera, or sera + anima | se-RA-mi | "Evening + day / soul". Three open syllables, easy in JA (セラミ). AR سيرة (*sīra*, life story) is inside it. | It is close to *ceramic*. The final stress (*Seramí*) is optional; the plain form *Serami* is safer. |
