# Root words from East and Southeast Asia

For an invented name for the evening-reflection journal (sun logo; themes: the day, light, body and mind, reflection, presence, calm, wholeness, memory, becoming).

Languages: Thai (TH), Japanese (JA), Korean (KO), Mandarin (ZH), Vietnamese (VI), Indonesian/Malay (ID/MS).

**What the rating means (1–5): how good the word is as a building block.** That means open syllables, no clusters, one obvious way to read the Latin spelling, and little baggage. A famous brand or a heavy sacred sense costs a point. It does **not** rate how good the word is as a finished name.

**What the brand checks cover.** Web searches on 24 Oct 2026, aimed at apps and wellness. They are not trademark searches. "None found" means only that a search turned nothing up.

### Notes that cut across the rows

- **Two Japanese words already do the blending for you.** *Yūhi* (夕日, evening sun) and *yūnagi* (夕凪, the evening calm at sea) are real words built from roots 5, 6 and 7. They match the product almost exactly: an evening that looks back on a day, under a sun.
- **The day words are the safest ground.** *Asa*, *hari*, *haru* and *hi* (and *ima*, "now") are all open CV syllables. Every language on the list can say them, and none has a bad meaning. The trade-off: *haru* is already a journaling-app name three times over.
- **Heart-mind words come in every language, and the famous ones are taken.** *Kokoro*, *maeum*, *xin* and *sati* are all crowded or hard to read. *Jai*, *hati* and *tâm* are short, free and pronounceable.
- **Reading the spelling is the hidden cost.** Pinyin *x*, *q* and *r*, Korean *eu*/*ae*, and Vietnamese *ng* all get misread by Latin-script readers. That is why most Mandarin and Korean roots score low even when their meaning is perfect.

## Eight suggested blends

None of these was brand- or trademark-checked. They are starting points.

| Blend | Built from | Reads as | Why it fits | Watch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Yunagi** | JA *yū* (evening) + *nagi* (calm) | yoo-NAH-gee | A real Japanese word for the evening calm. It describes the product's moment exactly | It is a dictionary word, so it may be in use in Japan; check |
| **Yuhi** | JA *yū* (evening) + *hi* (sun) | YOO-hee | The evening sun, which *is* the logo; two open syllables | A real word, short, likely used in Japan |
| **Nahari** | JA *nagi* (calm) + ID *hari* (day) | nah-HAH-ree | "A calm day." By luck it echoes Hebrew/Aramaic *nehara* (light) | An existing Hebrew surname (Nahari) |
| **Harima** | ID *hari* (day) + JA *ima* (now) | hah-REE-mah | "The day, now": presence plus the day | Harima is a historic Japanese province and a company name |
| **Jahari** | TH *jai* (heart) + ID *hari* (day) | jah-HAH-ree | "The heart of the day." It echoes the Johari window, a self-reflection model | Close to the Swahili name Johari ("jewel") |
| **Tamaya** | VI *tâm* (heart-mind) + ID *(caha)ya* (light) | tah-MAH-yah | "Heart-light." *Tamaya!* is what Japanese crowds shout at fireworks, a light in the night sky | The Tamaya fireworks house is historic; check JP marks |
| **Sabahi** | TH *sabai* (at ease) + JA *hi* (sun) | sah-BAH-hee | "At ease in the sun" | AR صباحي *sabāḥī* = "my morning", warm but the wrong time of day |
| **Hima** | JA *hi* (sun) + *ima* (now) | HEE-mah | "Sun, now." Two syllables | JA 暇 *hima* = free time (nice); Sanskrit *hima* = snow (Himalaya); likely crowded as a short brand |
