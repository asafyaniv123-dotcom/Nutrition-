# Olimei: the worldwide language and culture scan

Screened 24 Oct 2026. The name is **Olimei** (o-li-MAY), from Tamil ஒளி *oḷi* "light" and மெய் *mei* "body / truth".
The spelling variants **Olimay** and **Oli Mei** are checked alongside it.

This is a desk scan, not a native-speaker panel and not legal advice. It follows on from
`invented-r18.md` §1, which covered the trademark side: OLIMEI 0 marks, OLIMEY 0 marks, and OLIMEL as
Baxter's class 5 IV-nutrition product.

**How to read the marks**
- **VERIFIED**: confirmed this session against the source linked.
- **KNOWLEDGE**: from general linguistic knowledge. It was not looked up this session.
- **Severity**: NONE, then VERY LOW (a curiosity), LOW (a few people may notice), MEDIUM (needs a decision), HIGH (do not use).

### Tamil (the source)
| | |
|---|---|
| Reading | Written ஒளிமெய் it is *oḷi-mey*, with the retroflex ḷ. Written in Latin letters, most Tamils will default to ஒலி *oli* with the plain l, which means **"sound"** (VERIFIED, [Singapore Tamil Language Council word list](https://www.languagecouncils.sg/tamil/en/-/media/tlc/files/living-language-living-heritage-volume-1.pdf)). So "Olimei" is read as "sound-body". In English, "a sound body" is a happy accident. |
| As one word | ஒளிமெய் is not a dictionary word. No use of it was found anywhere, so it is not a film, a person or a brand (VERIFIED, by search). |
| Ambiguity | ஒளி is also the **verb "to hide"** (ஒளிந்து, as in hide-and-seek) (VERIFIED, the same Singapore list glosses ஒளி as both "light" and "hide"). Read as a verb-stem compound (*vinaittokai*), ஒளிமெய் could be parsed as "hidden truth / hidden body". Most readers will take "light" first. "Hidden truth" is poetic rather than negative, and it suits the brief, which wants a little hidden meaning (KNOWLEDGE for the grammar). |
| Nearest real thing | **ஒளிமயமான எதிர்காலம் *Olimayamāna Ethirkālam***, "a radiant future": a well-known T. M. Soundararajan song by Kannadasan from the film *Pachai Vilakku* (1964) (VERIFIED, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pachhai_Vilakku_(1964_film))). *Olimay-* will remind older Tamils of it, and the association is warm. *Mei* also names a consonant in Tamil grammar (KNOWLEDGE). |
| Severity | NONE. Note the ல / ள spelling choice for the Tamil-script logo: ஒளி, not ஒலி. |

## Part 3: every issue found, by severity

**No HIGH issues.** Nowhere is the name vulgar, sexual, insulting, a slur, sacred, or tied to a hate symbol.

**One issue needs a decision.**
1. **LOW–MEDIUM: the Chinese transliteration (Mandarin and Cantonese).** A 奥利- spelling borrows from 奥利给, a viral meme with a toilet backstory, and from 奥利奥 (Oreo). A playful 咩 ending reads as Cantonese "what?". **Fix:** register one official Chinese name now. 欧丽美 / 歐麗美 is clean and means "Europe · beautiful · beautiful". Do not let it be transliterated ad hoc. (VERIFIED)

**Low: a few people will notice.**

2. **Indonesian / Malay *oli* = motor oil.** "Oli Mei" sounds like "May engine oil". **Fix:** always one word, *Olimei*, and never *Oli Mei*. (VERIFIED)
3. **Nepal: *Oli* is the surname of K. P. Sharma Oli**, the PM ousted in Sept 2025. Nobody will think the app refers to him, but in Nepal *Oli* is a loaded word for now. (VERIFIED)

**Very low: curiosities, not risks.**

4. **English "limey"** sits inside the variant spellings *Olimey* / *Olimay*. It is a mild ethnic nickname for Britons. It does not appear in *Olimei*, and the stress differs. **Fix:** keep *-mei*; let *Olimay* exist only as a redirect domain. (VERIFIED)
5. **Persian / Urdu *mey* = wine**, the classical word. It is heard only if the name is split "Oli-mey". (VERIFIED)
6. **Tamil and Malayalam ஒளி / ഒളി also mean "hide".** ஒளிமெய் can be parsed as "hidden truth". Tamils reading Latin letters may also take *oli* as ஒலி "sound". The result is poetic, not negative. In Tamil script, write ஒளி, not ஒலி. (VERIFIED for the meanings; KNOWLEDGE for the grammar)
7. **Italian "oli miei" = my oils.** (VERIFIED)
8. **Near-names: OLIMEL** (Baxter IV nutrition) and **Captain Olimar** (Nintendo). These matter for search and trademark, not for meaning. (VERIFIED)
9. **Turkish *ölmeyi*** "dying" is a very distant echo that needs a different vowel. (KNOWLEDGE)
10. **Hebrew *olim*** "immigrants to Israel" is a pleasant word, close to politics. (VERIFIED)

## Verdict

**Globally safe. Clear to proceed**, with two conditions:
1. **Always write it as one word, *Olimei*.** "Oli Mei" opens the Indonesian motor-oil reading and looks like a person's name.
2. **Fix the official Chinese name (欧丽美 / 歐麗美) before launch in China, Hong Kong and Taiwan**, so that nobody uses 奥利- (the 奥利给 meme) or 咩.

About 35 languages and their major dialects were checked. None gives the name a vulgar, sexual, comic-in-a-damaging-way, insulting, religious or political meaning as a whole. The few echoes come from its halves (*oli*, *mei*) and are either neutral or pleasant. No famous person, character, song, film, place or brand is called Olimei, Olimay or Olimey.

**What this scan cannot promise.**
- Slang moves fast, and this was a desk scan.
- Before buying, a 10-minute check by one native speaker each of Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese, Arabic (Egyptian and Gulf), Indonesian and Tamil is still the cheapest insurance.
- The trademark registers named in `invented-r18.md` (EUIPO TMview, WIPO, J-PlatPat, the Israeli register) still need to be checked by hand.
