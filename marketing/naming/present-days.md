## 3. Meaning in English

- **"present day" means *nowadays*.** For a native speaker this is the dominant reading of the
  two words together: *present-day Britain*, *from Roman times to the present day*. It is a
  fixed phrase, and dictionaries own the search results for it.
- **The plural "present days" is unidiomatic.** Natives almost never say it. It reads either as
  a slightly off "present day", or as a coinage. That oddness is what *lets* the mindful
  reading in ("days in which you were present"), but the reader has to do the work.
- **"present" = gift** is the third reading. It is weaker in English than elsewhere, but real:
  a gift-planner app (*PresentDay*) and a 46-year gift shop (*Present Days*) both chose the name
  for that meaning.
- **"present days" = attendance** is a fourth reading, strong in Indian and HR English: the days
  an employee was marked present. Google Play's top results for the query are all attendance
  apps. Indian English speakers are a very large share of English-language Play users.
- **"being present" (mindful).** "Be present" and "stay present" are mainstream wellness
  phrases, and the app's sun logo supports them. But the mindful sense attaches to the
  *adjective after "be"*, not to "present" before a noun. Honest weighting for a cold native
  reader seeing "Present Days" alone: about 50% *nowadays* or odd phrase, 30% *being present*,
  20% gift, plus attendance in India.

**Steering.** The subtitle "Daily Reflection" helps a lot. "Present Days: Daily Reflection"
leans clearly toward the mindful reading, and kills the gift and attendance readings once a
person sees the whole title. A tagline can finish the job, for example *"Be present in your
days"*, *"Every day, fully present"* or *"A journal for the days you were there"*. The tagline
has to carry the word *be* or *there*: that is what converts "present" from time to presence.
In places where only the short name shows (the home-screen label "Present Days", the notification
sender), it will still read as "nowadays".

## Verdict: **NO-GO** (as it stands)

Section 1 alone would have been a GO: the US register is clean. The name fails on use. An iPhone
app called **Present Days**, in the next-door wellbeing category, with a subscription,
presentdays.app and an App Store Connect record, appeared in June 2026. Shipping a second
"Present Days" into the same store, four months behind it, invites a confusion claim we would
probably lose on priority. It would also have us competing for the exact search term from day
one, and BePresent (registered in class 9) is already standing next to both of us.

**What would turn it into GO WITH CARE:** confirm that the presentdays.app app is abandoned.
Its listing is 404 today; check again in a few weeks, write to hello@presentdays.app, or buy
the name from them. Then, separately, buy presentdays.com from the IONOS holder, and get a TMview
and WIPO search done by hand or by counsel.

### The three biggest risks

1. **A prior same-name app in the same category**: Present Days screen-time streaks,
   presentdays.app, App Store id 6779742843, since about June 2026. It also takes the .app
   domain. Add the 2026 iOS apps *Present Day* (365 dots, "proof you showed up") and
   *PresentDay* (gifts), and the name is getting crowded fast.
2. **The primary meaning is not ours.** For English natives "present day" means *nowadays*;
   Indian English and Play search read *attendance*; Japanese, Brazilian, German and school
   English read *gift*, and in Japanese a **giveaway campaign**. Only the subtitle rescues it,
   and the home-screen label has no subtitle.
3. **No clean owned assets.** presentdays.com (2004, gift shop), presentdays.app (competitor),
   presentdays.co (probably taken) and YouTube @presentdays are all gone. The non-US registers
   are unverified.

### Recommended handle (if the name goes ahead anyway)

**@presentdaysapp** on every network, with the domain **presentdaysapp.com** (free today).
Its YouTube handle is free. Instagram and TikTok must be checked by hand before committing. Do
**not** use @presentdays.app, which is the competitor's domain. Fallback: **@presentdaysjournal**,
which is free on YouTube and also says what the app is.
