# Twelve names, eleven languages

What each candidate does when a native speaker of each shipped language has to
**say** it from the Latin spelling, **hear** a meaning in it, and **type** it
into a store search box after hearing it once. Chinese is one row: Simplified
and Traditional readers say and search the name the same way; where Taiwan
differs, the row says so.

Notes that apply to every name, so they are not repeated in each table:

- **Arabic has no /p/ and no /v/.** Many speakers say p as b and v as f, and
  write پ/ڤ only when careful. A name with a p or v gets a second spoken form.
- **Spanish, French, Italian and Portuguese readers drop an initial h**, and
  none of the four (nor German, Japanese, Chinese, Arabic) know a silent w.
- **Japanese readers see any Latin syllable that is valid romaji as Japanese
  first** (`sake`, `kei`), before they see the English word.
- **Chinese users mostly search Latin brand names in Latin**, and coin a Chinese
  nickname only if the name hands them one. A name that translates cleanly into
  a warm Chinese phrase gets free word of mouth; one that doesn't stays Latin.
- **Nothing is wrong with `Sun` in Hebrew or Arabic slang**: סאן / سان carry no
  meaning in either. That worry can be dropped across the board.

Ratings judge the name for this product in all eleven markets, not English
alone.

## Sundot

| Language | Said / written | Meaning heard | Typed after hearing |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | "SUN-dot" | Says the logo literally: a sun used as a dot. The dot is a full stop, so "end" is there for anyone who has seen the icon. | Sundot |
| Hebrew | סאנדוט | Nothing in Hebrew | Sundot |
| German | "Sann-dott" (some say "Sun-dot") | Nothing. *Sund* (a strait) is too rare to surface. | Sundot |
| Spanish | "SUN-dot" | Nothing | Sundot |
| Japanese | サンドット | サン (sun) + ドット (dot), both everyday loanwords. サンド alone means sandwich, but ドット is common enough that the split reads サン・ドット. | Sundot |
| French | "seun-DOTT" | Nothing | Sundot |
| Italian | "SUN-dot" | Nothing | Sundot |
| Portuguese (BR) | "SUN-dótch" | Nothing | Sundot |
| Chinese | Latin; nickname 太阳点 if any | No ready Chinese phrase; stays Latin | Sundot |
| Arabic | ساندوت | Nothing | Sundot |

**Overall: STRONG.** Two words every market already knows, spelled the way
they sound, no meaning anywhere to trip on, and it *is* the logo. Its one
limit is that it says "sun", which leans morning; the evening has to come from
the icon and the product, not the word.

## Sunnote

| Language | Said / written | Meaning heard | Typed after hearing |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | "SUN-note" | A note written by the light of a sun: journal + mascot. Clear. | Sunnote, sometimes SunNote or Sunote |
| Hebrew | סאן-נוט | Nothing | Sunnote |
| German | "Sann-note" | *Note* is German too (a grade, a musical note). Neutral. | Sunnote |
| Spanish | "sun-NO-te" | *Nota* is close; "note" understood | Sunnote / Sunote |
| Japanese | サンノート | ノート = notebook, an everyday word. Very natural. | Sunnote |
| French | "seun-NOTT" | *Note* is French: a note. Reads as native. | Sunnote |
| Italian | "sun-NO-te" | *Note* = notes (plural of *nota*), one letter from *notte* = night. Both good. | Sunnote |
| Portuguese (BR) | "sun-NÓ-tchi" | *Nota*; Brazilians also call a laptop a *note*. Harmless. | Sunnote |
| Chinese | Latin; 太阳笔记 | 笔记 (notes) is the standard word for note apps, so 太阳笔记 is an easy nickname | Sunnote |
| Arabic | سان نوت | Nothing | Sunnote |

**Overall: STRONG.** Pronounceable everywhere, the "note" half is native or
near-native in French, Italian, German, Japanese and Portuguese, and Italian
hears *notte* for free. Two small costs: the double n is a light spelling trap
(Sunote), and "-note" is a crowded app suffix, so it sounds like a notes app
before it sounds like an evening.

## Daywrap

| Language | Said / written | Meaning heard | Typed after hearing |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | "DAY-rap" | "Wrap up the day" — the best English fit of the twelve | Daywrap, but also Dayrap |
| Hebrew | דייראפ | ראפ = rap music, if the w is dropped | Dayrap likely |
| German | "Day-wrapp" or "Day-vrap" | *Wrap* is known as the sandwich | Daywrap if seen, Dayrap if heard |
| Spanish | "dei-GWAP" / "dei-RAP" | Wrap the sandwich, or rap | Dayrap |
| Japanese | デイラップ | ラップ = cling film (サランラップ) or rap. "Day cling-film" is mildly comic. | Dayrap |
| French | "dé-RAP" | Rap | Dayrap |
| Italian | "dei-RAP" | Rap | Dayrap |
| Portuguese (BR) | "dei-HÉP" | Rap | Dayrap |
| Chinese | Latin | Nothing; no nickname | Dayrap |
| Arabic | دايراب ("day-rab") | Nothing | Dayrap / Dayrab |

**Overall: OK.** In English it carries the product perfectly. Everywhere else
the silent w means the name is *said* "day-rap" and *typed* Dayrap, and in
Japanese the wrap is plastic food wrap. Owning `dayrap` as well would be
necessary, not optional.

## Dayfin

| Language | Said / written | Meaning heard | Typed after hearing |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | "DAY-fin" | Fish fin. The "end" reading only lands for people who know *fin* from French films. Sounds faintly like *dolphin* or *Daphne*. | Dayfin |
| Hebrew | דייפין | Nothing | Dayfin |
| German | "Day-fin" | Nothing | Dayfin |
| Spanish | "dei-FIN" | *Fin* = end: "day-end". Also one sound from *delfín* (dolphin), which some will hear. Neither is bad. | Dayfin |
| Japanese | デイフィン | Nothing beyond the English | Dayfin |
| French | "dé-FIN" | *Fin* = end, and the film's closing card. A real bilingual pun. | Dayfin |
| Italian | "dei-FIN" | *Fine* = end; *fin* is the poetic/short form. Understood. | Dayfin |
| Portuguese (BR) | "dei-FÍN" | *Fim* = end, close enough to catch | Dayfin / Dayfim |

fin, and Arabic hears "buried". Easy to say and spell everywhere.

|---|---|---|---|

| Hebrew | בימלי | בים can read *ba-yam*, "in the sea" — harmless | Beamly / Bimly |

| French | "BIM-li" | Nothing | Bimly |

| Chinese | 比姆利 if transliterated; Latin otherwise | Nothing | Beamly |

fails in every language. It also leans on the mascot and says nothing about the
evening.

## Dayrest

| Language | Said / written | Meaning heard | Typed after hearing |

| Hebrew | דיירסט | Nothing | Dayrest |

## Hushday

| Language | Said / written | Meaning heard | Typed after hearing |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | "HUSH-day" | Quiet, calm, end of noise. Good fit. | Hushday |
| Hebrew | האשדיי | Nothing | Hushday / Hashday |
| German | "Husch-day" | ***Husch*** = quick, scurry off ("husch, husch!") — the opposite of calm | Huschday |
| Spanish | **"USH-dei"** (h silent; *sh* is foreign) | *Hush* not known | Ushday |
| Japanese | ハッシュデイ | ハッシュ = hash (#, hashtag, hash browns). "Hash Day". | Hashday |
| French | **"OUCH-dé"** (h silent) | *Hush* not known | Hushday if seen, Ouchday heard |
| Italian | **"USH-dei"** (h silent) | Not known | Ushdei |

## Daysake

| Language | Said / written | Meaning heard | Typed after hearing |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | "DAY-sake" | "For the day's sake" — an idiom in pieces; awkward. Many will read *sake* the drink. | Daysake |
| Hebrew | דייסייק | Read "daysa-ke" it starts with דייסה, porridge. Faintly comic. | Daysake |
| German | "Day-SA-ke" | ***Sake* = rice wine** — the loanword is far better known than the English idiom | Daysake |
| Spanish | "dei-SA-ke" | *Sake* = rice wine | Daysake |
| Japanese | デイサケ, not デイセイク | ***酒 sake* = alcohol.** "Day-sake" is 昼酒, daytime drinking, on an app opened in the evening. Also 鮭, salmon. The most off-message reading in the set. | Daysake |
| French | "dé-sa-KÉ" | *Saké*, the drink | Daysake |
| Italian | "dei-SA-ke" | *Sakè*, the drink | Daysake |
| Portuguese (BR) | "dei-SA-ki" | *Saquê*, the drink | Daysake |
| Chinese | Latin; some will read 清酒 | Drink, for those who know the loan | Daysake |
| Arabic | دايسيك | Nothing; the drink for those who know it | Daysake |

**Overall: WEAK.** In seven of eleven languages, *sake* is rice wine, and in
Japanese it is simply *alcohol*. An evening ritual app whose name reads
"day's drink" will be read that way.

## Summary

| Name | Rating | Key reason |
|---|---|---|
| Sundot | STRONG | Says the logo, sayable and spellable in every market, no bad meanings |
| Sunnote | STRONG | "Note" is native in FR/IT/DE/JA/PT; Italian hears *notte*; crowded "-note" suffix |
| Daywrap | OK | Best English meaning; silent w makes it "Dayrap" in ten languages, cling film in Japanese |
| Dayfin | OK | Real "end" pun in ES/FR/IT/PT; English hears a fish fin, Arabic "buried" |
| Smallsun | OK | 小太阳 is a gift in Chinese; Hebrew hears *smol* (left/leftist); flat in English |
| Sunkeep | OK | Clean everywhere, keepsake idea; nothing about evening |
| Beemly | WEAK | Typed "Beamly" or "Bimli" everywhere after hearing |
| Tonightly | WEAK | English only; unsayable and unspellable in the Romance markets |
| Dayrest | OK | Calm in English; "leftovers" in German and the Romance languages |
| Hushday | WEAK | h silent in four markets, *hash* in three, *husch* (hurry) in German |
| Daysake | WEAK | *Sake* = rice wine in seven languages, alcohol in Japanese |
| Evenlit | OK | Says evening light; typed "Evenlight", and *lit* = drunk in US slang |
